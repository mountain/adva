# Square-Map Branch, Quotient, and Copy Calibration

Status: exploratory finite calibration extending
[`0038-triadic-characteristic-inference-calibration.md`](0038-triadic-characteristic-inference-calibration.md)
and using the checked finite program infrastructure documented in
[`PROGRAM_PROCESS_CORE.md`](../PROGRAM_PROCESS_CORE.md).

This note tests the next pressure point proposed in `0038`:

\[
Q(x)=x^2.
\]

The affine examples in `0038` were deterministic, injective, and
single-branch. The square map is still deterministic in the forward temporal
direction, but it introduces three new phenomena at once:

1. temporal information loss, because \(Q(x)=Q(-x)\);
2. spatial branching, because the inverse image of a positive interval has
   two connected components; and
3. constructive fan-out, because the checked term for \(x^2\) uses an explicit
   copy before multiplication.

The principal result is deliberately two-level.

> The common triadic characteristic scheme survives the square-map test:
> temporal evaluation, spatial inverse image, and constructive realization
> still identify one extensional map and still obey the variance signature
> \((+,-,+)\).

However:

> The binary-affine characteristic pair \((k,b)\) is not closed under the
> square generator. A full branch-resolved and provenance-preserving
> characteristic can no longer be represented by one plain affine monoid
> element. It requires at least a layered carrier: an extensional map or graph
> correspondence, a finite branch symmetry and critical-set decoration, and a
> checked constructive copy/multiply witness with residual history.

This is a research note and bounded test, not a stable Adva API, a generic
observer pullback, a general semialgebraic-set implementation, a proof that
all program characteristics are spans, or a claim in `claims.toml`. The
spatial inverse images below are exact mathematical fixtures over selected
rational intervals. They are not the reserved `D*` and do not create program
identity. Rust remains the authority for program, source, occurrence, history,
slice, and certificate identity.

The note follows the project format: intuition, finite evidence and structural
formalization, conservative conclusion, and red-team opinion.

## 1. Intuition

### 1.1 Why the square map is the first real pressure test

The affine calibration used

\[
S(x)=x+1,
\qquad
D(x)=2x.
\]

Every finite word generated by `S` and `D` had the form

\[
x\longmapsto 2^k x+b,
\]

and could therefore be compressed into one pair

\[
(k,b).
\]

The map \(Q(x)=x^2\) changes the problem qualitatively.

It is not injective. Its reverse state transport is not a function. Its open
inverse images can split. Its construction requires two occurrences of one
source. It also interacts differently with scaling and translation:

\[
D_a;Q \equiv Q;D_{a^2},
\]

but

\[
S_b;Q
\]

cannot be moved past `Q` while remaining inside an affine post-composition.
The translation crossing creates the cross term \(2bx\).

Thus `Q` tests four boundaries of the earlier method:

- whether a common characteristic can survive information loss;
- whether contravariant spatial interpretation can retain branches;
- whether constructive interpretation must expose explicit copy; and
- whether order reduction remains a word rewrite or becomes a graph rewrite.

### 1.2 Temporal information loss is a finite group quotient

Let

\[
q:\mathbb R\longrightarrow\mathbb R,
\qquad
q(x)=x^2.
\]

Introduce the involution

\[
\sigma(x)=-x.
\]

Then

\[
q\circ\sigma=q.
\]

The temporal observation that retains only the square value induces

\[
x\sim_q x'
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
q(x)=q(x').
\]

Over the real line,

\[
x\sim_qx'
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x'=x
\ \text{or}\
x'=-x.
\]

Thus the generic observer fibre is one orbit of the finite group

\[
C_2=\{1,\sigma\}.
\]

The map factors through the orbit quotient:

\[
\mathbb R
\xrightarrow{\pi}
\mathbb R/C_2
\xrightarrow{\cong}
[0,\infty)
\hookrightarrow
\mathbb R.
\]

This is the first calibration in which controlled forgetting is not merely a
general principle. The forgotten distinction is exact and finitely
presented: sign is quotiented by a two-element symmetry.

The origin is exceptional. It is fixed by \(\sigma\), while every nonzero orbit
has two elements. The finite quotient therefore has a non-free point.

### 1.3 Spatial branching is the same quotient read backwards

Restricting away from the origin gives

\[
q:\mathbb R^\times\longrightarrow\mathbb R_{>0}.
\]

This is a two-sheeted covering map. The involution \(\sigma\) exchanges the two
sheets.

For a positive open interval

\[
U=(a,b),
\qquad
0<a<b,
\]

the inverse image is

\[
q^{-1}(U)
=
(-\sqrt b,-\sqrt a)
\;\cup\;
(\sqrt a,\sqrt b).
\]

The two components are exchanged by reflection.

When a target neighborhood crosses the critical value \(0\), the two spatial
sheets meet in the source. For example,

\[
q^{-1}((-1,1))=(-1,1).
\]

The spatial observer therefore sees more than a radius change. It sees:

- the number of connected inverse-image components;
- their order and signs;
- a branch-exchange involution;
- a source critical point \(x=0\); and
- a target critical value \(y=0\).

The source critical point and target critical value must remain distinct
typed objects even though both are represented by the scalar `0` in this
example.

### 1.4 Reverse value transport is a relation, not a function

The forward temporal map is single-valued:

\[
x\longmapsto x^2.
\]

The reverse state fibre is

\[
q^\dagger(y)
=
\{x\in\mathbb R:x^2=y\}.
\]

Hence

\[
q^\dagger(y)
=
\begin{cases}
\varnothing, & y<0,\\
\{0\}, & y=0,\\
\{-\sqrt y,\sqrt y\}, & y>0.
\end{cases}
\]

A plain inverse function does not exist globally.

There are two safe ways to retain this fact.

The first is to keep the forward function and interpret space through inverse
image on sets. This is sufficient for unlabelled open-set semantics:

\[
q^{-1}:\mathcal O(\mathbb R)\longrightarrow\mathcal O(\mathbb R).
\]

The second is to retain the graph correspondence

\[
\Gamma_q
=
\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^2:y=x^2\}
\]

with its two projections

\[
\mathbb R
\xleftarrow{s}
\Gamma_q
\xrightarrow{t}
\mathbb R,
\]

where

\[
s(x,y)=x,
\qquad
t(x,y)=y.
\]

The graph contains both the deterministic forward map and the multi-valued
reverse fibres. Composition can then be represented by a fibre product of
graph correspondences.

The first carrier is enough for extensional forward evaluation and unlabelled
open inverse images. The second becomes useful when branch identity or
reverse-state composition is part of the characteristic.

### 1.5 Constructive squaring is an explicit fan-out followed by multiplication

The constructive realization is not the textual abbreviation `x*x` with
implicit aliasing. In the current finite linear Adva core it is explicitly

```lisp
(mul (copy (use x)))
```

Schematically,

\[
x
\xrightarrow{\Delta_2}
(x_-,x_+)
\xrightarrow{\mu}
x_-x_+.
\]

The two output occurrences of `copy`:

- are distinct occurrences;
- retain the same source;
- have distinct lineage paths; and
- are consumed by multiplication.

This distinction is essential. Value equality does not authorize source
identification, occurrence contraction, common-subexpression elimination, or
sharing.

The constructive characteristic therefore has two layers:

1. the extensional value map \(x\mapsto x^2\); and
2. a checked witness that one source was explicitly copied into two
   occurrences and then multiplied.

The second layer cannot be reconstructed from the extensional polynomial
alone.

### 1.6 The three appearances of “two” must not be identified prematurely

The square calibration contains three binary structures:

1. two generic temporal states \(x\) and \(-x\) in one quotient fibre;
2. two spatial inverse-image sheets over a positive target; and
3. two constructive occurrences produced by explicit copy.

They are compatible in this example, but they are not automatically the same
mathematical object.

The temporal pair comes from a symmetry orbit. The spatial pair comes from a
covering fibre. The constructive pair comes from a fan-out operation in a
checked program diagram.

A future theorem may relate these structures under a declared interpretation,
but the calibration must first keep them typed and separate. Their common
cardinality is evidence for a possible bridge, not proof of an identity.

### 1.7 The affine normal form fails exactly at translation crossing

Let

\[
D_a(x)=ax,
\qquad
S_b(x)=x+b.
\]

Using the chronological notation of `0038`, `p;q` means execute `p` first and
`q` second.

Scaling crosses the square map by a parameter deformation:

\[
D_a;Q
\equiv
Q;D_{a^2}.
\]

Indeed,

\[
Q(D_a(x))
=
a^2x^2
=
D_{a^2}(Q(x)).
\]

Translation does not admit the analogous crossing inside the old affine
language:

\[
Q(S_b(x))
=
(x+b)^2
=
x^2+2bx+b^2.
\]

Suppose there were an affine map

\[
A(y)=\alpha y+\beta
\]

such that

\[
S_b;Q
\equiv
Q;A.
\]

Then

\[
(x+b)^2
=
\alpha x^2+\beta.
\]

For \(b\ne0\), the left side has a nonzero linear coefficient \(2b\), while the
right side has none. Therefore no such affine `A` exists.

The failure is not merely that a parameter pair becomes larger. Pushing a
translation through a square changes the shape of the construction:

\[
(x+b)^2
=
x^2+2bx+b^2.
\]

An extensional expansion requires parallel branches and explicit fan-out.
Schematically,

\[
x
\xrightarrow{\Delta_3}
(x_1,x_2,x_3)
\xrightarrow{Q\otimes D_{2b}\otimes C_{b^2}}
(x_1^2,2bx_2,b^2)
\xrightarrow{\operatorname{add}_3}
x^2+2bx+b^2.
\]

Thus order reduction has crossed a structural boundary:

\[
\boxed{
\text{word rewrite}
\longrightarrow
\text{typed DAG rewrite with explicit copy and merge}.
}
\]

This is the first exact obstruction to extending the affine `S;D` crossing
mechanism unchanged.

### 1.8 The candidate characteristic is layered

A minimal branch-aware research carrier can be written schematically as

\[
\widehat Q
=
(
\Gamma_q,s,t;
\sigma;
R_q,B_q;
\kappa_q
),
\]

where:

- \(\Gamma_q\) is the graph of \(q\);
- `s` and `t` are its source and target projections;
- \(\sigma(x,y)=(-x,y)\) is the branch-exchange involution;
- \(R_q=\{0\}\) is the source critical or ramification set;
- \(B_q=\{0\}\) is the target critical-value set; and
- \(\kappa_q\) is a checked constructive copy/multiply witness.

This is a finite symbolic presentation. It does not enumerate all values,
opens, or histories.

For tasks that need only forward values and unlabelled open inverse images,
the extensional core `q` may be sufficient. For tasks that need reverse
states, branch identity, critical structure, or construction provenance, the
decorations are operational rather than optional.

### 2.2 Exact temporal recovery inside the quadratic class

Let

\[
f(x)=ax^2+bx+c.
\]

Write

\[
y_{-1}=f(-1),
\qquad
y_0=f(0),
\qquad
y_1=f(1).
\]

Then

\[
y_{-1}=a-b+c,
\]

\[
y_0=c,
\]

and

\[
y_1=a+b+c.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
c=y_0,
\qquad
a=\frac{y_1+y_{-1}-2y_0}{2},
\qquad
b=\frac{y_1-y_{-1}}{2}.
}
\]

For the observed table

\[
(-1,1),
\qquad
(0,0),
\qquad
(1,1),
\]

the recovered characteristic is

\[
(a,b,c)=(1,0,0).
\]

The equality

\[
y_{-1}=y_1
\]

certifies the vanishing linear coefficient within the quadratic class. It is
also a finite witness of evenness at the selected probes, but it is not by
itself a theorem that an unrestricted function is globally even.

Let

\[
U=(a,b),
\qquad
a<b.
\]

The square inverse image is

\[
q^{-1}(U)
=
\{x:a<x^2<b\}.

For the cases used by the calibration:

\[
q^{-1}(a,b)
=
\begin{cases}
\varnothing,
& b\le0,\\[2mm]
(-\sqrt b,\sqrt b),
& a<0<b,\\[2mm]
(-\sqrt b,0)\cup(0,\sqrt b),
& a=0<b,\\[2mm]
(-\sqrt b,-\sqrt a)\cup(\sqrt a,\sqrt b),

\end{cases}
\]

Two exact probes are especially informative.

The positive probe

\[
U_+=(1,4)

has

q^{-1}(U_+)

The crossing probe

U_0=(-1,1)

has

\[
q^{-1}(U_0)=(-1,1).
\]

The first observes two sheets. The second observes their merger through the
critical source point because the target includes the critical value.

A finite spatial record should not store only the union. For the split probe

\[
(

\sigma_X

where

\[
U_-=(-2,-1),
\qquad
U_+=(1,2),

and

\[
\sigma_X(U_-)=U_+,
\qquad
\sigma_X(U_+)=U_-.
\]

The graph

\[
\Gamma_q=\{(x,y):y=x^2\}
\]

provides a common extensional object.

Temporal evaluation is read from the graph by the unique target associated
with each source:

\[
x
\longmapsto
t(s^{-1}(x)).
\]

Spatial inverse image is

\[
q^{-1}(U)
=
s(t^{-1}(U)).
\]

Reverse state fibres are

\[
q^\dagger(y)
=
s(t^{-1}(y)).
\]

The involution

\[
\widetilde\sigma(x,y)=(-x,y)
\]

acts on the graph and satisfies

\[
t\circ\widetilde\sigma=t.
\]

This supplies one finite diagram from which the temporal value map, spatial
open pullback, and reverse relation can all be read without pretending that
the reverse relation is a function.

### 2.6 Triadic compatibility survives

Define the three interpretations of the square characteristic.

Temporal:

\[
\rho_t(\widehat Q)(x)=x^2.
\]

Spatial:

\[
\rho_X(\widehat Q)(U)=q^{-1}(U).
\]

Constructive:

\[
\rho_K(\widehat Q)(e)
=
\operatorname{mul}(\operatorname{copy}(e)).
\]

Then the evaluation square commutes:

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{eval}
(
\rho_K(\widehat Q)(e)
)
=
\rho_t(\widehat Q)
(
\operatorname{eval}(e)
).
}
\]

The open-membership square also commutes:

\[
\boxed{
x\in\rho_X(\widehat Q)(U)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\rho_t(\widehat Q)(x)\in U.
}
\]

For sequential composition with another characteristic `f`,

\[
\rho_t(\widehat Q\odot f)
=
\rho_t(\widehat Q)\circ\rho_t(f),
\]

while

\[
\rho_X(\widehat Q\odot f)
=
\rho_X(f)\circ\rho_X(\widehat Q).
\]

The spatial interpretation therefore remains contravariant. The finite
variance signature is still

\[
\boxed{(+,-,+)}.
\]

What changes is not the variance law but the structure required to retain
branch identity and constructive provenance.

### 2.9 Why a plain endomorphism monoid is both sufficient and insufficient

There are two different questions.

For forward values and unlabelled open inverse images, a plain function

\[
q\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb R)
\]

is sufficient. Inverse image is functorially determined by the function.

For branch-resolved reverse states, critical-set tracking, and construction
provenance, the bare function value does not expose enough operational
structure. One must either recompute that structure from a richer declared
function theory or retain it in the characteristic and residual.

Thus the safe conclusion is not

> ordinary functions fail,

nor

> every characteristic must be a span.

It is:

> a plain function is the extensional core of this calibration; a full
> triadic operational characteristic requires additional typed branch and
> construction data.

### 2.10 Scaling crossing survives

For nonzero rational or real \(a\), define

\[
D_a(x)=ax.
\]

Then

\[
D_a;Q
\equiv
Q;D_{a^2}.
\]

This gives a deformed crossing law inside the extended language. In
particular,

\[
D_2;Q
\equiv
Q;D_4.
\]

The bounded test compiles both checked histories and verifies that they have
the same SymPy value while retaining different histories.

This is structurally similar to the affine crossing

\[
S;D\equiv D;S^2
\]

from `0038`: a generator crosses another generator by changing a parameter.

### 2.11 Translation crossing forces branching

For \(b\ne0\),

\[
S_b;Q
\]

cannot be represented as

\[
Q;A
\]

for an affine `A`.

The coefficient obstruction is exact. Every post-affine square has form

\[
\alpha x^2+\beta
\]

and zero linear coefficient, while

\[
(x+b)^2=x^2+2bx+b^2
\]

has nonzero linear coefficient.

The only extensional expansion in the arithmetic language introduces a
parallel decomposition. For \(b=1\),

\[
S;Q
\equiv
Q+2I+1.
\]

But `Q+2I+1` is not one temporal word in the old one-input/one-output
generator monoid. It denotes a DAG that:

1. copies the input into multiple occurrences;
2. computes `Q`, `D`, and a constant branch;
3. adds the branch outputs; and
4. preserves source and occurrence provenance.

This is the exact point at which a monoid of flat words becomes too small and
a typed diagrammatic algebra becomes necessary.

### 2.12 Arithmetic circuits are a safer finite characteristic carrier than expanded polynomials

Once `Q` and `S` coexist, polynomial degree and expanded representation can
grow rapidly.

Consider

\[
P_n=S;Q^n.
\]

Its extensional value is

\[
P_n(x)=(x+1)^{2^n}.
\]

A nested square circuit has size linear in `n`: one increment followed by `n`
square stages.

The fully expanded polynomial has

\[
2^n+1
\]

nonzero monomials:

\[
(x+1)^{2^n}
=
\sum_{j=0}^{2^n}
\binom{2^n}{j}x^j.
\]

Therefore an expanded polynomial coefficient vector is not a generally safe
normal form for the characteristic. A checked shared arithmetic circuit or
DAG can be exponentially smaller.

This does not prove that every circuit equivalence can be decided efficiently.
It establishes the narrower representation rule:

\[
\boxed{
\text{retain compact circuit structure;}
\quad
\text{do not require expanded polynomial materialization.}
}
\]

The bounded test instantiates four nested square calls after one increment.
The checked call history has five calls, while the expanded value
\((x+1)^{16}\) has seventeen nonzero terms.

### 2.13 Bounded algorithmic consequences

Within this exact fixture, the calibration has concrete algorithmic
advantages.

**Quadratic inference.** Three fixed rational probes recover the coefficients
with a constant number of arithmetic operations.

**Exact branch extraction.** For intervals with rational-square endpoints,
the split or merged inverse-image components are computed symbolically without
sampling.

**Reverse fibres.** Selected target values have exact finite reverse fibres
rather than numerically discovered roots.

**Circuit compression.** Nested squaring remains a short checked program even
when polynomial expansion becomes exponentially larger.

**Cross-domain pruning.** Temporal coefficients, spatial component structure,
and constructive copy provenance provide independent constraints. A candidate
that fits one projection can be rejected by another.

These advantages are conditional. A richer hypothesis class, arbitrary
semialgebraic regions, or unrestricted circuit equivalence can be much more
expensive.

## 3. Conservative conclusion

### 3.1 What the calibration supports

Within the declared finite real quadratic fixture, the square-map calibration
supports the following claims.

1. One common extensional characteristic can still be read temporally,
   spatially, and constructively.
2. The triadic compatibility equations and variance signature `(+,-,+)`
   survive non-injectivity.
3. Temporal information loss is exactly described by a finite \(C_2\) orbit
   quotient.
4. Spatial inverse images require finite unions of components and can carry a
   branch-exchange involution.
5. Reverse state transport is naturally relation-valued even though forward
   execution remains functional.
6. Constructive squaring requires an explicit checked copy and retains
   occurrence provenance not recoverable from the polynomial.
7. The old binary-affine pair `(k,b)` is not closed under adjoining `Q`.
8. Scaling admits a deformed crossing through `Q`, while translation does not
   remain inside an affine post-normal form.
9. Translation crossing changes a sequential word into a branched arithmetic
   DAG.
10. Compact circuit form can avoid exponential polynomial or tree expansion.
11. A complete result must retain characteristic, certificate, and residual.

The revised finite pattern is

\[
\boxed{
\text{function or graph core}
+
\text{branch symmetry and critical sets}
+
\text{checked construction witness}
+
\text{certificate and residual}.
}
\]

### 3.2 The main theoretical revision

The affine calibration suggested that a characteristic might often be an
element of a finite-parameter endomorphism monoid.

The square calibration refines this:

> A finite characteristic is not necessarily a flat monoid element. It may be
> a compact typed diagram whose extensional shadow is a function, whose
> spatial reading is contravariant and branch-aware, and whose constructive
> reading retains explicit fan-out and source provenance.

In categorical language, the smallest adequate syntax is moving from one
endomorphism monoid toward a typed symmetric monoidal or PROP-like diagram
language with explicit copy, parallel composition, and merge. This note does
not select a final categorical ontology or promote such a structure to the
stable API.

### 3.3 Consequence for order reduction

The affine example gave a successful deformed crossing:

\[
S;D\equiv D;S^2.
\]

The square example gives a mixed result:

\[
D_a;Q\equiv Q;D_{a^2}
\]

but no affine crossing for

\[
S_b;Q.
\]

Instead, the latter expands into a branched DAG.

Therefore a general left/right or forward/reverse reduction cannot be assumed
to be a total sorting procedure on program words. A more realistic target is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{local crossing laws}
+
\text{controlled graph expansion}
+
\text{compressed DAG representation}
+
\text{residual when closure fails}.
}
\]

This is a sharper and more conservative lesson for the later surreal `L/R`
problem. The present calibration does not solve that problem.

### 3.4 What is not established

The note does not establish that:

- arbitrary Adva programs admit finite graph characteristics;
- graph correspondences or spans are the final universal carrier;
- every inverse-image branch can be finitely represented;
- branch multiplicity equals constructive copy multiplicity;
- the \(C_2\) quotient is observer-independent;
- exact real radicals are available for arbitrary interval endpoints;
- generic quadratic or polynomial inference is efficient;
- arithmetic-circuit equivalence is decidable by the proposed method;
- a branch-aware observer pullback exists in the stable Rust core;
- a program rewrite to expanded arithmetic is source- or occurrence-
  preserving;
- the current fixture implements open-cover gluing, sheaves, or a full cut
  calculus; or
- the square calibration determines the startup alignment of the three
  computers.

### 3.5 Immediate next research steps

The next bounded sequence should be:

1. add exact branch identifiers and branch-transition certificates to a
   research-local region fixture;
2. compose `Q` with one translation and one scaling in both orders;
3. compare graph-composition branch data with direct symbolic composition;
4. test `x^3` to separate real reverse-fibre count from constructive copy
   multiplicity;
5. test `x^2-1` or `x^2+c` to separate the source critical point from a shifted
   target critical value;
6. test two-variable multiplication, where inverse images are hyperbolic
   rather than finite unions of intervals;
7. retain the circuit rather than expanding it, and measure characteristic,
   certificate, and residual sizes separately; and
8. only then decide whether a research-level `CharacteristicGraph`,
   correspondence, or branch-aware region type is justified.

## 4. Red-team opinion

### 4.1 The quadratic hypothesis class supplies most of temporal identifiability

Three probes uniquely recover a quadratic because the observer has already
declared degree at most two. The same table is compatible with infinitely many
unrestricted functions.

The correct result is therefore:

\[
\text{unique within } \mathcal F_2,
\]

not globally unique.

### 4.2 Exact interval endpoints provide unusually strong spatial information

The interval probe records exact endpoints and connected components. A coarser
observer that records only nonemptiness, measure, radius, or connectivity
would identify less structure.

The success of this fixture must not be generalized to arbitrary spatial
observers.

### 4.3 Branch labels are additional observer structure

The unlabelled set

\[
(-2,-1)\cup(1,2)
\]

does not by itself say which component is `negative`, which is `positive`, or
how later charts transport those labels. The branch involution is a declared
decoration.

A chart change can permute branch names while preserving the same extensional
open set. Certificates must distinguish intrinsic branch relations from
presentation labels.

### 4.4 A function already determines inverse images

One might object that no graph correspondence is needed: the function
\(x^2\) already determines every inverse image.

That objection is correct for unlabelled set semantics. The graph carrier is
motivated only when reverse fibres, branch identities, relation composition,
or critical data are operational outputs. The note must not inflate a useful
presentation into an ontological necessity.

### 4.5 Copy multiplicity and covering degree coincide only accidentally here

The square term copies one source twice, and the positive real inverse fibre
has two points. This numerical match is suggestive but not general.

For example, a more complicated quadratic circuit can still contain several
copy operations while its generic real fibre has zero or two points depending
on the target. Conversely, representation choices can alter copy count
without changing polynomial degree.

No theorem should identify the two quantities without additional hypotheses.

### 4.6 The critical source and critical target must remain typed

Both are `{0}` for \(x^2\), which makes accidental conflation easy.

They have different roles:

\[
R_q=\{x:q'(x)=0\}
\]

is a subset of the source, while

\[
B_q=q(R_q)
\]

is a subset of the target.

A later map such as \(x^2+c\) separates their numerical locations.

### 4.7 The real-line calibration omits infinity and complex branching

As a polynomial map on the Riemann sphere, \(z\mapsto z^2\) also has branching
at infinity. The present real bounded fixture sees neither that compactified
point nor complex phases.

The result is local to the declared real observer.

### 4.8 Circuit compression does not solve circuit equivalence

A short arithmetic circuit can denote a polynomial with exponentially many
terms. Retaining the circuit avoids expansion, but comparing two arbitrary
circuits can still be difficult.

Representation compression and semantic decision complexity are separate
questions.

### 4.9 The test reads checked construction but models space in Python

Rust certifies the program diagram, copy slice, occurrences, source lineage,
evaluation, and differential rules. The exact interval pullback helper is a
research-local mathematical model in Python.

It is evidence for the finite formula, not a certified Adva spatial pullback.

### 4.10 A failed common carrier would still be progress

The next examples may show that one graph-plus-decoration format cannot serve
all three domains without awkward or unbounded residuals.

That would not invalidate characteristic inference. It would establish that
the common object is a relation among typed domain-specific characteristics
rather than one carrier with three interpretations. The square calibration
narrows this question but does not settle it.
