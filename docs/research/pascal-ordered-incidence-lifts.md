# Two ordered lifts of a Pascal incidence

*Research note · 21 November 2026 · v0.1*

Mingli Yuan (苑明理) provided the research direction. Codex (OpenAI) authored the analysis and checks, contributed under Unknown v0.3, and submitted this note through Mingli Yuan's GitHub account as an authorized proxy.

Michael Harris's *Do Androids Prove Theorems in Their Sleep?* asks what mathematical understanding amounts to even when every step can be verified. Here is a small instance in which a change of expression has a precise cost. A normalized proof of Pascal's theorem gives two expressions for the final incidence. Both vanish over the usual commutative coefficient ring. Keeping the order of multiplication exposes different conditions for their vanishing, and two $2\times2$ matrix assignments distinguish them in both directions. [1, §8]

## The normalized configuration

Use homogeneous coordinates on $uw=v^2$ and take the six ordered points
$$
 A=(1,0,0),\quad B=(0,0,1),\quad C=(1,1,1),\quad
 D=(1,d,d^2),\quad E=(1,e,e^2),\quad F=(1,f,f^2).
$$
Over $\mathbb Q$, take $d,e,f$ distinct and outside $\{0,1\}$. The intersections of opposite sides $AB\cap DE$, $BC\cap EF$, and $CD\cap FA$ are represented by
$$
 X=(1,0,-de),\quad Y=(1,1,e+f-ef),\quad Z=(1+d-f,d,df).
$$
The line through $X$ and $Y$ has row $\ell=(de,-t,1)$, with $t=de+e+f-ef$. Pascal's conclusion in this presentation is $\ell Z^\mathsf{T}=0$. The coordinates and their supplied incidence checks are recorded in the Adva Pascal witness. [2]

Now read the same written expressions in the free associative algebra $\mathbb Z\langle d,e,f\rangle$. Treat points as columns and evaluate line coefficients on the left. With $[x,y]=xy-yx$, the literal evaluation and the commutatively rewritten display of that evaluation give
$$
\begin{aligned}
 R_{\mathrm{left}}&=de(1+d-f)-td+df
       =[d,e](1-f)+(1-e)[d,f],\\
 R_{\mathrm{display}}&=de(1+d-f)-dt+df
       =-d[d,e].
\end{aligned}
$$
Expanding the right-hand sides gives the ordered words on the left. Both expressions lie in the commutator ideal, and their difference is $[d,t]$.

The full presentation makes a stronger statement than either final incidence alone. It has six conic equations, twelve endpoint incidences, six incidences defining the intersections, and three incidences with the Pascal line. Adva's existing certificate records four nonzero residuals in the free algebra. [3]

| Equation | Residual |
|---|---|
| $D$ on $DE$ | $[d,e]$ |
| $E$ on $EF$ | $[e,f]$ |
| $Z$ on $FA$ | $[d,f]$ |
| $Z$ on $\ell$ | $[d,e](1-f)+(1-e)[d,f]$ |

It follows that in any unital associative ring, the 27 fixed equations hold exactly when $d,e,f$ commute pairwise. The first three rows give necessity, and the fourth row together with the 23 identities gives sufficiency. The calculation concerns a fixed system of ordered polynomial equations. The projective interpretation above uses the rational field.

## Two matrix assignments

Set $N=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$ and take
$$
 d=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&3\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 e_x=\begin{pmatrix}4&x\\0&5\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 f=\begin{pmatrix}6&-2\\0&7\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The matrices $d,e_x,f$, their differences from the identity, and their pairwise differences are all invertible. This follows from their nonzero diagonal entries. Since $[d,e_x]=-xN$ and $[d,f]=2N$, the two residuals are
$$
 R_{\mathrm{left}}=6(x-1)N,\qquad R_{\mathrm{display}}=2xN.
$$
At $x=1$, the literal incidence holds and the display fails. At $x=0$, the display holds and the literal incidence fails. Thus the two vanishing tests are incomparable, even with these invertibility conditions. Equivalently, the two-sided ideals generated by the respective residuals in the free algebra are incomparable. If one residual belonged to the ideal of the other, every substitution killing the generator would kill both.

These assignments also let us distinguish an algebraic rewrite from a coordinate change. For an invertible $3\times3$ matrix $U$ over the coefficient ring, simultaneous transport of a point $p$ and a line $L$ by $p\mapsto Up$ and $L\mapsto LU^{-1}$ preserves the ordered incidence residual $Lp$ exactly. Knowing which changes have this property tells a reader more than knowing that both original expressions reduce to zero in $\mathbb Z[d,e,f]$.

## Reproduction

The pinned checker reconstructs the 27 equations and the separately supplied auxiliary display from the original task and witness. It expands signed ordered words without importing the generator. Replay with the pinned library inputs reproduced the retained certificate, check and negative-control outputs byte for byte. The two matrix assignments above are a separate exact calculation in $M_2(\mathbb Q)$. [2, 3]
