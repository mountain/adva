# Exponential Expression Transport Across Causal Cuts

Status: bounded Python research construction over Rust-checked finite diagrams.
This extends the expression-valued cut-composition witness by one checked
analytic builtin.  It is not a stable exp-polynomial type, observer module,
matrix calculus, or `D*` API.

## Question

The non-exponential optical experiment established an identity-preserving
composition law for expression-valued demands on nested causal cuts.  The next
question is whether that law was an accident of polynomial arithmetic, or
whether it survives the first operation traditionally lying outside the
polynomial class.

The `exp` test must not be cosmetic.  It should satisfy three requirements:

1. a cut demand must genuinely depend on an exponential intermediate
   expression;
2. the same unsimplified expression must compose across every checked cut; and
3. numerical evaluation must agree independently with Rust differentiation
   and the numerical endpoint witness.

## The exponential optical program

Let the checked parameter cell produce

$$
\begin{aligned}
y_0&=(1-\kappa)x+s,\\
y_1&=-\kappa x+s.
\end{aligned}
$$

Attach the nonlinear checked readout

$$
r(y_0,y_1)=\exp(y_0)+y_1.
$$

The resulting scalar program is

$$
\mathcal E(x,s,\kappa)
=
\exp\bigl((1-\kappa)x+s\bigr)-\kappa x+s.
$$

Unlike a fitted numerical objective, this program retains the factorized
device history and places `exp` strictly in the future of the two-port optical
cut.

## Local exponential rule

For a forward argument expression $A$ and an output demand expression $Q$,
the bounded reverse rule is

$$
\operatorname{exp}:\qquad
Q\longmapsto Q\,\exp(A).
$$

The witness deliberately performs no simplification.  Therefore the demand
generated from the constant probe $1$ is recorded as

$$
1\cdot\exp(A),
$$

not silently replaced by $\exp(A)$.  This retains the construction that the
output probe passed through an exponential event.

The numerical research rule uses the corresponding evaluated coefficient
$Q\exp(A)$.  Both are independently compared with Rust's authoritative
forward structural differential.

## The exponential cut section

At the cut between the parameter cell and the nonlinear readout, the scalar
output probe $1$ becomes

$$
q_{\mathrm{optical\ cut}}
=
\bigl(1\cdot\exp(y_0),1\bigr).
$$

This is the first tested cut whose probe is not constant and whose coefficient
depends on a complete intermediate program expression.  Both cut endpoints
carry all three sources $x,s,\kappa$.  Their occurrence lineages nevertheless
have different sizes, three and four, because the position output contains an
additional occurrence of the shared $x$ source.

Thus equal source support does not collapse occurrence geometry, even when
the observer coefficient is nonlinear.

## Root expression family

Write

$$
Z=\exp\bigl((1-\kappa)x+s\bigr).
$$

Evaluation of the unsimplified backward expressions gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_x&=(1-\kappa)Z-\kappa,\\
Q_s&=Z+1,\\
Q_\kappa&=-x(Z+1).
\end{aligned}
$$

These three expressions agree with the Rust gradient of $\mathcal E$ at all
three declared numerical fixtures.  More strongly, every symbolic endpoint
demand evaluates to the corresponding numerical backward endpoint demand,
with identical checked lineage and source decorations.

## Exponential cut composition

The experiment reuses the same identity-preserving segment construction.  For
every nested pair of completed pasts $U\subseteq V$ in the exponential
composite diagram, it compares:

- one uninterrupted backward traversal; and
- the outer, middle, and inner traversals through $\partial V$ and
  $\partial U$.

At every endpoint the two constructions produce exactly equal unsimplified
`DemandExpression` syntax.  Consequently

$$
\mathcal B_{U\leftarrow W}
=
\mathcal B_{U\leftarrow V}
\circ
\mathcal B_{V\leftarrow W}
$$

survives the checked `exp` extension throughout this finite fixture.

The result has a useful structural meaning:

> `exp` changes the local coefficient language, but it does not change the
> causal-cut carrier or its composition mechanism.

## Polynomial-like versus exp-polynomial

The experiment now supports a more disciplined terminology.

### Native level

The native object under test is a program-like expression transport.  Its
coefficient language is generated by the checked operations admitted by the
program.  Adding `exp` extends that language by one local constructor without
changing endpoints, occurrence sections, or composition.

In this broad generative sense, `polynomial-like` may reasonably include
exponential terms: the word describes a recursively generated expression
family rather than the classical ring of polynomials.

### Commutative Real chart

After forgetting occurrence construction and choosing the usual commutative
Real interpretation, the resulting coefficients belong to an exp-polynomial
or exponential-polynomial class.  That is a derived analytic classification,
not the definition of the native carrier.

Therefore

$$
\text{polynomial-like}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\text{exp-polynomial}
$$

can coincide only after declaring what closure and chart the first term means.
They are not unconditionally synonymous.

## Why a matrix-like form also appears

Although the forward program is nonlinear in $(x,s,\kappa)$, every local
backward rule is linear in the incoming probe $Q$.  After choosing finite input
and output port charts, the transport can therefore be tabulated as a
matrix-like action on probes.  Its entries are no longer constant scalars:
they are program expressions such as $\exp(y_0)$.

This gives a precise bounded version of the proposed identification:

> the polynomial-like coefficient presentation and the matrix-like probe
> presentation are two readings of one expression-valued cut transport.

They have the same generated coefficients and the same composition law, but
they retain different declared interfaces.  No triangular matrix or Jordan
form is needed to obtain this relation.

## Consequence for the spectral carrier

The carrier remains

$$
\boxed{
\text{occurrence-decorated expression sections over causal cuts}
}
$$

now with an exponential coefficient language.  A matrix with exp-polynomial
entries can be compiled after selecting a probe basis.  Its eigenvalues would
be observations of repeated transport in that chart; they would still not
replace the underlying section carrier.

## Executable evidence

The extended optical test verifies:

1. checked execution and native differentiation of the nonlinear exponential
   objective;
2. the presence of the versioned `adva.builtin:exp@1` rule in both symbolic
   traversal and Rust's differential certificate;
3. exact root-gradient formulas at three numerical fixtures;
4. endpoint-by-endpoint agreement between symbolic and numerical backward
   fields, including lineage and source decoration;
5. exhaustive direct-versus-three-segment expression equality over every
   nested pair of causal opens in the exponential composite;
6. a nontrivial optical cut demand of raw form
   $1\cdot\exp(y_0)$;
7. dependence of that cut coefficient on all three original program inputs;
   and
8. distinct occurrence-lineage sizes despite equal three-source support at the
   two optical cut endpoints.

## No-go boundaries

This calibration does not establish:

- closure under arbitrary analytic, elementary, or special functions;
- a canonical exp-polynomial normal form or equality decision procedure;
- that every object called polynomial-like must contain `exp`;
- symbolic observer variables or a general proof of probe linearity;
- a stable matrix-like, covector, section, functor, or `D*` object;
- composition across separate programs or alternative expression schedules;
- a matrix standard form, program spectrum, learning law, or physical model.

## Next obligation

The next experiment should replace the fixed numerical probe by independent
symbolic probe generators.  Transport should then be shown to be additive and
homogeneous in those generators at every cut.  Coefficient extraction would
produce a matrix-like shadow whose entries are precisely the polynomial-like
or exp-polynomial program expressions already carried by the section.

That test would address the proposed polynomial-like/matrix-like identity
directly, without making a matrix the prior ontology.
