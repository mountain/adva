## 1. The question

The question put to this repository, in its own terms, was whether C. C. Lin's
density-wave theory can be brought in, on the ground that

> there is essentially a rotational, inward-contracting force that maintains the
> long-time stability of the structure, and it might be expressed through a
> scalar spatial fluctuation.

That reading has three separable parts, and they do not have the same answer:

1. **rotation supplies a restoring tendency**;
2. **the restoring tendency is an inward contraction**;
3. **the mechanism can be carried by a scalar spatial fluctuation**.

Part 1 is right and is exactly the epicyclic frequency. Part 2 is wrong about
which term does what. Part 3 is right about the *field* and wrong about the
*operator*: the density is indeed a scalar, but the term that makes the theory
work is not local, and no local scalar equation can produce it. The rest of this
note separates those three, and computes the parts that are exact algebra.

## 2. What the source theory actually claims

Stated plainly, and imported rather than proved: spiral arms are not material
arms that would wind up under differential rotation, but a **quasi-stationary
density wave** rotating rigidly at a **pattern speed** distinct from the local
angular velocity of the disk. Its local form, in the tight-winding approximation,
is a dispersion relation for the radial wavenumber `k` at azimuthal number `m`,

```
(omega - m Omega)^2 = kappa^2 - 2 pi G Sigma |k| + c_s^2 k^2,
```

with `Omega` the angular velocity, `kappa` the epicyclic frequency, `Sigma` the
surface density and `c_s` a sound speed or velocity dispersion. The sources are
[Lin & Shu 1964](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1964ApJ...140..646L/exportcitation),
[Toomre 1964](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1964ApJ...139.1217T) for the
stability parameter,
[Goldreich & Lynden-Bell 1965](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/130/2/125/2600980)
for swing amplification,
[Lynden-Bell & Kalnajs 1972](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/157/1/1/2604585)
for the angular-momentum argument, and the book by
[Bertin & Lin](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262023962/spiral-structure-in-galaxies/)
for the modal development. A useful independent exposition is Tremaine's
[lecture on disk dynamics and spiral structure](https://www.ias.edu/sns/tremaine/lectures/ast513/spiral).

Two consequences of the source theory matter for the reading above, and both
point against part 2 of it.

**Gravity's term is destabilising, not contractive.** In the relation above the
self-gravity term enters with a minus sign in front of `|k|`. It lowers the
squared frequency and is what can drive the disk unstable; the stabilising term
is the pressure term `c_s^2 k^2`. So self-gravity is the term that makes the disk
want to clump, not a force pulling the structure inward.

**The inward flow is a consequence, not the cause.** Density waves transport
angular momentum outward and mass inward (Lynden-Bell & Kalnajs). That is a real
and central result, but it is a *secular* effect that evolves the disk; it is not
what makes the pattern persist. What makes the pattern persist is that the pattern
rotates rigidly, so it is not wound up by the shear that acts on the material, and
that the disk sits near a marginal condition rather than deep inside stability.

**And the persistence itself is not a settled result.** Whether a quasi-stationary
spiral is a genuine global mode or a pattern continually regenerated by swing
amplification is a long-standing question in the theory rather than a theorem this
note can invoke. That is *my* reading of the literature, it is not verified by any
computation below, and it means the phrase "maintains long-time stability" imports
an open question. The same caution as with the working name "HNS equation" in note
0169 applies.

## 3. The reading, checked part by part

**Part 1 is right, and it has a name.** The restoring tendency supplied by
rotation is the epicyclic frequency `kappa`, defined by
`kappa^2 = r d(Omega^2)/dr + 4 Omega^2`. A displaced fluid element oscillates
about its circular orbit at `kappa` rather than falling in. In the dispersion
relation `kappa^2` sits exactly where a restoring term belongs. So the intuition
that rotation supplies the restoring structure is correct, and the exact quantity
is `kappa`. The run confirms the classical values for a declared family of
power-law rotation curves with `Omega^2` proportional to `r^n`, `n = -2a`:

| rotation exponent `a` | `n` | `kappa^2 / Omega^2` | epicyclic real |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 0 (solid body) | 0 | 4 | yes |
| 1/2 | −1 | 3 | yes |
| 1 (flat curve) | −2 | 2 | yes |
| 3/2 (Keplerian) | −3 | 1 | yes |
| 2 | −4 | 0 | **no** |

and it confirms a classical coincidence exactly: for this family the epicyclic
frequency is real **if and only if** the specific angular momentum `r^2 Omega`
increases with radius, which is the Rayleigh criterion. Both signs are computed
independently and compared for every declared exponent.

**Part 2 is wrong.** There is no inward-contracting term in the relation. What is
true, and what the intuition may be reaching for, is stated exactly in section 4.5:
self-gravity is *two-sided* — bounded above for local stability and bounded below
for the wave to cross the corotation barrier — so one quantity both permits and
constrains the structure. That is a window, not a contraction.

**Part 3 is half right.** The density is a scalar field, and calling it one is
correct. But the term that makes it a *density wave* is `-2 pi G Sigma |k|`, and
section 4.3 shows that no polynomial symbol equals `|k|`, so no local
constant-coefficient scalar operator carries it. A scalar is enough if it is
allowed to be **nonlocal** — coupled through a Poisson solve, which is what
`Sigma -> Phi -> Sigma` is — and provably not enough if the scalar must obey a
local equation.

## 4. What was computed

All acceptance arithmetic is exact: rationals, plus a hand-written quadratic-surd
field for `sqrt 2`. `G` and `pi` never appear, because the self-gravity
coefficient is declared as a single rational `g` standing for `2 pi G Sigma`; that
physical identification is imported and never checked. 91 rows of the quadratic,
27 rows of the window, 91 rows of the tunnelling threshold, 96 grid points, 7
interpolations, 2533 assertions, 0.04 seconds.

### 4.1 The marginal wall is a discriminant

On `k >= 0` the modulus is just `k`, so the relation is a genuine quadratic in the
radial wavenumber,
`f(k) = c_s^2 k^2 - g k + kappa^2`, with discriminant
`Delta = g^2 - 4 c_s^2 kappa^2 = (g - 2 kappa c_s)(g + 2 kappa c_s)`.
Its minimum over `k >= 0` is `-Delta / (4 c_s^2)`, and the run compares three
independently computed quantities: the sign of the minimum, the sign of `Q - 1`
with `Q = 2 kappa c_s / g`, and the sign of `-Delta`. They agree on every row.

- `Q > 1`: no real root, no unstable wavenumber, stable at every wavelength.
- `Q = 1`: a **double root**, and it sits exactly at `k = kappa / c_s`.
- `Q < 1`: two roots, both strictly positive.

So Toomre's criterion is not an analogy for the discriminant of the Lin–Shu
relation; it *is* the discriminant condition, and the wall is a zero of a
discriminant rather than a boundary drawn by hand. The exact wall row
`g = 2 kappa c_s` is added to the grid deliberately for every declared
`(kappa, c_s)`, so the wall is tested at zero distance rather than approached by a
sweep: 9 such rows, each with minimum exactly zero.

### 4.2 Existence and geometry of the band are set by different data

When `Q < 1` the unstable set is a **bounded band** `k_1 < k < k_2`, strictly
away from both `k = 0` and `k = infinity`: the disk is stable at long wavelengths
and stable again at short ones. The two edges have exact symmetric functions,
`k_1 + k_2 = g / c_s^2` and

```
k_1 k_2 = kappa^2 / c_s^2,
```

which contains no `g` at all. The run verifies this by grouping all band rows by
`(kappa, c_s)` and checking that the product is a single value across every
self-gravity coefficient tested: 9 groups, one value each. So the **geometric mean
of the band edges is fixed by rotation and sound speed alone**, while
self-gravity decides whether the band exists at all and how wide it is. One datum
sets existence, another sets extent.

### 4.3 The modulus, and a finite certificate that it is not local

Two surrogates built from the same declared data are run as refusal controls.
Dropping the modulus term gives `kappa^2 + c_s^2 k^2`, whose minimum is
`kappa^2 > 0` for **every** row: no instability at any wavelength, no band, ever.
Replacing `|k|` by `k^2` gives `kappa^2 + (c_s^2 - g) k^2`, which is either stable
everywhere or unstable at **arbitrarily short** wavelengths — no upper edge, hence
never a band. Only the modulus produces a band bounded at both ends.

That is a symptom. The reason is exact. A symbol of a local constant-coefficient
operator is a polynomial in `k`, and no polynomial agrees with `|k|` on a two-sided
neighbourhood of the origin. The general argument is two lines: if `p` agrees with
the modulus on an interval of positive numbers then `p - k` has infinitely many
roots there and vanishes identically, so `p(k) = k`, which is negative where the
modulus is positive. The run checks the algebraic core of that over degrees 0 to 6:
matching the modulus at `n+1` distinct positive rational nodes determines a unique
polynomial of degree at most `n`, and for every `n` in the declared range that
polynomial is exactly `k`, with value `-3` at `-3` where the modulus is `3`.

So the term that makes the disk a self-gravitating medium rather than a
wave-bearing local field is **certifiably nonlocal at the level of polynomial
symbols**. This is a statement about the term, not about the physics: a nonlocal
scalar completion is not excluded, and is in fact the correct reading.

### 4.4 The resonance skeleton, exactly

For the flat rotation curve `Omega = V/r`, the epicyclic frequency is `kappa =
sqrt(2) Omega`, and with `omega = m Omega_p` the relation
`(omega - m Omega)^2 - kappa^2 = 0` has roots

```
r = r_cr (1 - sqrt(2)/m)   and   r = r_cr (1 + sqrt(2)/m),
```

the inner and outer Lindblad radii about the corotation radius `r_cr = V/Omega_p`.
The run computes these as elements of `Q(sqrt 2)` and verifies four things
exactly, with no floating point:

- substituting each radius into the frequency relation gives exactly zero, so the
  Lindblad radii **are** the turning points where the wave changes between
  propagating and evanescent;
- corotation is the exact arithmetic midpoint of the two, for every `m`;
- the inner radius exists exactly when `m^2 > 2`, checked by two independent
  routes, so `m = 1` has none and `m >= 2` has both — for `m = 1` the radius
  formula gives `1 - sqrt(2) < 0`, and the entire region inside the outer
  resonance is evanescent;
- over a declared rational grid the sign of the frequency relation is positive
  outside and negative between the two radii, which is the evanescent band.

### 4.5 The same discriminant in a second role, and a window

Inside the evanescent band the frequency relation is negative. Writing its depth
as `c^2 = kappa^2 - (omega - m Omega)^2 > 0`, a real radial wavenumber requires

```
c_s^2 k^2 - g k + c^2 = 0   to have real roots,  that is   g >= 2 c_s c.
```

That is the same discriminant condition as the marginal wall, and it reads in the
**opposite direction**: the local instability wall wants `g` *small*, and crossing
the barrier wants `g` *large*. So self-gravity is confined to a window

```
2 c_s c  <=  g  <=  2 kappa c_s,
```

non-empty exactly when the barrier depth is below `kappa^2`, of width
`2 c_s (kappa - c)`, and empty when the barrier is deeper than that. The run
computes the window for 27 declared rows: 18 open, 6 closing to a point, 3 empty.

This is the exact form of the intuition in part 2 of section 1. One quantity both
sustains and forbids the structure, and the structure is possible only in a
two-sided window rather than under a force.

### 4.6 The window closes at corotation, at Toomre's value

Push that one step further and the answer is sharper than expected. At corotation
the matter term equals the frequency, so the barrier depth is exactly `kappa^2`,
and the lower threshold `2 c_s c` becomes `2 c_s kappa`, which **is** the local
stability wall. The window closes.

The run establishes the depth rather than asserting it: it computes the epicyclic
frequency from the rotation curve, checks its square is `2 (V/r_cr)^2` with no
surd part left, reads the barrier depth from the frequency relation at `r_cr`, and
verifies that the square root of that depth is exactly `kappa` by squaring. So at
corotation the marginal-stability threshold and the barrier-crossing threshold
coincide, at the Toomre value, for every declared `m` and `c_s`.

## 5. Two kinds of wall

Note 0169's finite model has stability walls too: the parameter loci where the
Harder–Narasimhan filtration jumps. It is worth saying exactly how the two differ,
because the difference is checkable and the resemblance is not an identity.

- The **density-wave wall is a degeneracy**. Crossing `Q = 1` changes the number of
  real wavenumbers from zero to two: a multiplicity event, at a double root.
- The **Harder–Narasimhan wall is a tie**. Crossing it changes only which subobject
  has the maximal slope. Every object and every filtration is unchanged — the
  sibling run's class set is recomputed here from its two dimension bounds alone
  and reproduces the reported 19 classes, and the parameter appears in its contract
  only as declared lists, so no count can depend on it. Its own evidence, pinned by
  sha256 `09280cf0…`, is read and never modified.

Both walls are codimension one and both are decided by exact arithmetic. One is
born from a double root and the other from an equality of two linear forms between
which the ordering flips. **The shape of a wall is shared; the wall is not.** The
conflation is registered as forbidden in `docs/claims.toml`.

## 6. Why a scalar shadow cannot carry a mechanism — the same lesson twice

The previous three rounds ended with a refutation of exactly the same shape as
section 4.3. In note 0169 the graded shadow — dimension pairs and slopes — was
supposed to pin an object, and it does not: the Harder–Narasimhan filtration
destroys the rank, recording the rank per piece does not repair it because the
pieces agree as objects, and the residual turns out to be an extension class. A
scalar summary was taken for the mechanism and the mechanism was not in it.

Density wave theory supplies an independent instance, this time from physics and
this time with a proof instead of a counterexample: the field is a scalar, the
mechanism is the `|k|` term, and `|k|` is not a polynomial symbol, so a **local**
scalar cannot carry it. Two unrelated questions, one failure mode, and in both
cases the failure was a shadow standing in for the word it came from. That is now
three places in this corpus where the same mistake has a name — the graded shadow,
the local scalar, and the older shadow-versus-word line — and it is worth treating
the pattern itself as the finding rather than any one instance. It is offered here
as an observation about method, not as a theorem, and no two of the three are
claimed to be instances of a single principle.

## 7. The ladder, and what is refused

This is a separate ladder; call it `D`. Nothing on it is a rung of `R` from note
0169, and the only thing that crosses is the shape of a wall, as section 5 states.

- **D0, done here.** The local dispersion relation as exact algebra: the marginal
  locus as a discriminant, the band and its two edges, the modulus as a certified
  nonlocal term.
- **D1, done here.** The turning points: exact Lindblad radii, exact evanescence,
  and the two-sided window that closes at corotation.
- **D2, refused.** The self-consistency problem: the density produces the
  potential which produces the density again, and a pattern speed must be found
  that makes this a fixed point. This is a nonlinear eigenvalue problem for a
  nonlocal operator, and nothing in this repository can check it. It is also the
  honest reading of "reflexive" here — the pattern is defined by its own output —
  and the corpus's `ReflexiveLatticeGate` is a different, finite thing.
- **D3, refused.** Angular-momentum transport with a direction and the secular
  evolution it drives. It needs a global wave and a dissipative medium.
- **D4, refused.** Anything observational. Nothing here observes a galaxy.

The imported relation is the whole of the import. No part of it is derived,
verified, or justified by this run, and no coefficient of it is checked against
data.

## 8. Errors recorded in this round

Three, all caught by the checks rather than by review, and all recorded rather
than smoothed:

1. **`Omega` used where `m Omega` was meant.** The turning-point test conflated
   two different quantities: the matter term is `m Omega(r)`, while the epicyclic
   term is `kappa(r)`, which for the flat curve is `sqrt(2) Omega(r)` with no
   factor of `m`. The first version wrote both as `Omega`, which coincidentally
   gives the right answer at `m = 1` — the first arm tested — and hid the error
   until `m = 2`. This is the fourth error of this series in which a quantity was
   not the one its name said, and the first in which a special case made it
   invisible rather than merely wrong.
2. **A wrong acceptance criterion, written into the contract before the run.**
   The first version of the contract asserted that a surrogate replacing `|k|` by
   `k^2` "fails to produce any unstable wavenumber". That is false: for `g` above
   `c_s^2` it produces instability at arbitrarily short wavelengths. The contract
   was corrected before the run to say what is true — no bounded band, ever — and
   the correction is visible in the committed contract.
3. **A cross-experiment check that assumed a shape instead of measuring it.** The
   first version asserted that only one parameter-bearing key exists in the sibling
   contract; there are two (`theta_sweep` and `degenerate_theta`). The check now
   enumerates the keys and verifies that the class count is a function of the
   dimension bounds alone, which is what the contrast actually needs.

## 9. What this does not establish

- No galaxy is modelled, no global mode is constructed, no spiral structure is
  predicted, and no structure is claimed to be stable for a long time.
- The dispersion relation is imported. The agreement of the Euler-type identities
  in section 4.1 is a check on this implementation, not a derivation of the
  relation or of Toomre's criterion.
- The modulus nonlocality is certified for **constant-coefficient polynomial
  symbols**. A nonlocal scalar completion is not excluded, and is the correct
  reading of the source theory.
- The power-law family checks exponent bookkeeping and the coefficient identity;
  the differentiation of a Laurent monomial is the power rule by definition, so
  nothing about the existence of the derivative is verified.
- The two-sided window and its closure at corotation are statements about the local
  relation at declared parameters. Whether a real disk satisfies them, and whether
  the window is ever non-empty there, is not addressed.
- The wall comparison of section 5 compares shapes. No transfer of theory, no
  common theorem and no identification of the two walls is claimed.
- No Arakelov, Monge–Ampère, mirror or Calabi–Yau content, exactly as in note 0169
  section 5. No native Rust witness, no library admission, no Seal.
