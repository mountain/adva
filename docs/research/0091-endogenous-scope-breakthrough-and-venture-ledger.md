# Endogenous Scope Breakthrough and the Venture Ledger

Status: working research synthesis following
[0080](0080-finite-surface-universal-lift-imagination.md),
[0088](0088-historical-distributivity-character-v0.md),
[0089](0089-failure-frontiers-observer-relative-closure.md), and
[0090](0090-prefix-frontier-closure-calibration-plan.md).

This note records a correction initiated by Mingli Yuan:

> Learning a pattern inside a fixed scope does not by itself break the
> observer's finitude. A finite observer breaks its present scope only when it
> internally proposes an admissible combination outside the scope currently
> certified, tries it under a finite budget, records the cost and outcome, and
> allows the result to change the vocabulary used by later proposals.

No stable API, semantic identity, learner, proof system, energy semantics,
universality result, or right-to-forget judgment is introduced here.

---

## 0. The missing distinction

Notes 0089 and 0090 answer an important but narrower question:

\[
\text{When may one feature close while the represented world remains open?}
\]

Their answer is observer-relative invariance over all retained legal fillings.
That protects a finite observer from pretending that an open world is fully
known.

It does not answer:

\[
\text{How does the observer produce a possibility not already contained in
its current certified scope?}
\]

The two achievements must remain distinct.

| achievement | exact question | possible result |
|---|---|---|
| feature closure | can every currently legal filling agree on one feature? | stable finite vocabulary |
| scope breakthrough | can the observer generate, test, and retain a candidate beyond that vocabulary's current certified use? | an enlarged effective language |
| object closure | is the complete represented object unique? | much stronger and usually unavailable |

A closed feature may make the observer more stable while leaving it trapped in
one fixed grammar. Conversely, an out-of-scope venture may fail and still be
valuable because it changes the frontier, constraints, costs, or future
proposal order.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{FeatureClose}
\ne
\operatorname{Breakthrough}.
}
\]

## 1. Three degrees of novelty

The word novelty also needs a scope index.

### 1.1 Empirical novelty

A new instance was absent from the observed sample but already lies inside the
current certified scope.

This tests generalization. It does not change the observer's language.

### 1.2 Scope novelty

A candidate lies outside the domain on which the current characteristic or
historical character is certified, while remaining expressible and type
checkable in a declared ambient constructor language.

This is the first genuine venture.

### 1.3 Vocabulary novelty

A successful scoped construction is objectified as a reusable historical
character, and later candidate generation can use that character as a
constructor.

This changes the observer's effective language and search geometry. The new
word may still macro-expand into an older universal metalanguage, so the
change need not increase Turing computability. It can nevertheless change
description length, discoverability, compositional reach, and the finite cost
of asking the next question.

The proposed finite breakthrough requires scope novelty followed by an
auditable possibility of vocabulary novelty. Merely seeing a new datum is not
enough.

## 2. Breakthrough is relative, not absolute

No effective formal observer can prove that it has stepped outside every
possible metalanguage. Candidate generation itself must have a finite rule.

Let:

- \(G_t\) be the observer's current effective grammar;
- \(S_t\) be the exact scope certified for its current characters;
- \(\operatorname{Adm}(G_t)\) be the ambient finitely generable and
  type-checkable candidate space.

An out-of-scope candidate satisfies

\[
c_{t+1}
\in
\operatorname{Adm}(G_t)
\setminus
\operatorname{Scope}(S_t).
\]

This is a relative escape from the current certificate, not an absolute escape
from syntax.

Open-endedness is likewise potential rather than completed:

1. every actual candidate, trial, certificate, and ledger entry is finite;
2. no individual run contains an infinite search;
3. the generation rule has no fixed global semantic-depth field; and
4. under a declared fair schedule, every finitely generated admissible
   obligation can eventually receive finite attention.

A bounded test suite can check the construction law at increasing depths. It
cannot prove that every semantically meaningful novelty will be found.

## 3. Observer state with a venture history

The earlier finite surface and failure-frontier records should be extended
conceptually, not replaced.

A minimal research state is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal O_t
=
\left(
G_t,
S_t,
A_t,
H_t,
\Lambda_t
\right),
}
\]

where:

- \(G_t\) is the effective grammar currently available for proposal and
  composition;
- \(S_t\) is the family of scope and characteristic certificates;
- \(A_t\) is the structured unresolved failure frontier;
- \(H_t\) is the library of reusable historical characters, with residuals
  and reopen handles; and
- \(\Lambda_t\) is the ordered venture ledger.

These components have different authority.

- \(G_t\) makes a candidate expressible.
- \(S_t\) says where current reasoning is certified.
- \(A_t\) records where reasoning fails, remains open, or encounters tension.
- \(H_t\) carries successfully objectified constructions.
- \(\Lambda_t\) records what was actually tried and paid for.

Equal grammars do not imply equal scopes. Equal scopes do not imply equal
histories. Equal scalar cost does not identify ledgers.

## 4. The three imagination operators revisited

Note 0080 proposed

\[
J_K=\operatorname{FreshWitness},
\qquad
J_X=\operatorname{Split},
\qquad
J_t=\operatorname{FairExtend}.
\]

The present correction makes their out-of-scope obligations explicit.

| operator | local action | breakthrough obligation |
|---|---|---|
| \(J_K\) | construct a fresh typed witness, patch, combination, or candidate character | at least some proposals must cross the current certificate boundary |
| \(J_X\) | retain alternative sites, fillings, placements, schedules, or possible worlds | alternatives must not be collapsed before the trial observer is declared |
| \(J_t\) | fairly advance still-admissible finite trials | timeout must not become refutation, and no fixed global depth may define the whole future |

In compressed form:

\[
\boxed{
J_K\text{ proposes new combinations},
\quad
J_X\text{ preserves their alternatives},
\quad
J_t\text{ gives them finite chances}.
}
\]

Random mutation is not sufficient. A useful proposal must carry:

1. an origin in a witnessed failure, open aperture, retained residual, or
   existing historical character;
2. a derivation in the declared constructor grammar;
3. a type or boundary admissibility check;
4. an exact statement of which current scope boundary it crosses; and
5. a finite trial budget.

Imagination grants candidacy, not truth.

## 5. The venture transition

A single venture has the form

\[
\operatorname{Venture}
(\mathcal O_t,c,b,Q)
\longrightarrow
(o,\mathcal L,\mathcal O_{t+1}),
\]

where \(c\) is a generated candidate, \(b\) is a finite budget, \(Q\) is the
trial observer, \(o\) is a typed outcome, and \(\mathcal L\) is the new ledger
entry.

The transition should factor into auditable stages:

\[
\operatorname{Failure}
\to
\operatorname{Generate}
\to
\operatorname{Admit}
\to
\operatorname{Trial}
\to
\operatorname{Characterize}
\to
\operatorname{Record}
\to
\operatorname{PromoteOrRetain}.
\]

Generation and checking must not be conflated. A Python research generator may
propose syntax, but present Adva authority still requires Rust-owned checking
for programs, types, sources, occurrences, histories, and certificates.

A venture may change the observer even when it does not produce an accepted
character:

- a counterexample may add a constraint;
- an open result may refine \(A_t\);
- nonrepresentability may expose a missing constructor or boundary type;
- fuel exhaustion may change a later scheduling decision;
- implementation failure may invalidate the trial apparatus;
- a successful characteristic may become eligible for promotion.

No outcome permits silent deletion of the candidate trace or its cost.

## 6. Outcome vocabulary

The first result algebra should keep at least these cases separate.

| outcome | meaning | permitted state change |
|---|---|---|
| `Accepted` | the declared finite trial and characteristic obligations pass | retain witness and consider scoped promotion |
| `Refuted` | a checked counterexample defeats the candidate claim | add witnessed constraint or residual |
| `Open` | represented legal alternatives still disagree or remain unresolved | retain or refine the aperture |
| `NotRepresentable` | the requested candidate or certificate is outside the current research carrier | expose a language or representation boundary |
| `FuelExhausted` | the finite schedule ended without a logical verdict | record cost and reschedule or retain |
| `CertificateObstruction` | the claim may hold, but coverage, invariance, or provenance evidence is missing | refuse promotion |
| `ImplementationFailure` | parser, generator, checker, or harness behavior is unreliable | quarantine the result and repair the apparatus |

In particular,

\[
\operatorname{NotRepresentable}
\ne
\operatorname{Refuted},
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{FuelExhausted}
\ne
\operatorname{SemanticFailure}.
\]

A system that records only successful trials cannot learn the shape or cost of
its own finitude.

## 7. The venture ledger

The cost requested in the proposal must first be a structured causal record,
not one reward number.

A candidate ledger entry is

\[
\mathcal L_i
=
\left(
\operatorname{id}_i,
\operatorname{origin}_i,
c_i,
\operatorname{generation}_i,
\operatorname{crossing}_i,
Q_i,
b_i,
\mathbf C_i,
\tau_i,
o_i,
R_i,
\Delta A_i,
\Delta S_i,
\Delta G_i,
\operatorname{promotion}_i
\right).
\]

It should contain:

- a fresh venture identity;
- the originating Failure aperture, residual, or character;
- exact candidate syntax and typed boundary;
- the finite generation derivation;
- evidence that the candidate was outside the previous certified scope;
- the trial observer and modality;
- requested budget and actual consumption;
- ordered execution and checking trace;
- typed outcome;
- retained residuals and counterexamples;
- frontier and constraint changes; and
- any scoped vocabulary promotion.

The first cost carrier is the three-domain vector

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf C_i
=
\left(
C_i^K,
C_i^X,
C_i^t
\right),
}
\]

where:

| component | first finite reading |
|---|---|
| \(C_i^K\) | constructors, program nodes, fresh witnesses, proof obligations, and generated artifact size |
| \(C_i^X\) | retained alternatives, frontier width, occurrence sites, live fillings, and peak storage |
| \(C_i^t\) | evaluation events, search steps, schedules advanced, checker calls, and fuel |

A later energy model may evaluate

\[
E_i
=
E(\mathbf C_i,\tau_i,R_i,\text{platform}),
\]

but it must not retroactively replace the structured record. Equal energy can
hide different construction, space, time, failure, and provenance histories.

Opportunity cost, discarded alternatives, external measurements, and
amortized reuse should be additional declared fields when available. They
must not be invented by the semantic kernel.

## 8. Promotion changes the effective language

An accepted trial is not automatically a new primitive.

A scoped promotion should have the form

\[
\operatorname{Promote}
(c,\chi,\operatorname{ScopeCert},R,H)
\longrightarrow
\sigma,
\]

where \(\sigma\) is a historical character carrying:

- its checked body or presentation correspondence;
- the observer-relative characteristic;
- the exact certified scope;
- source-to-instance provenance;
- complete retained process residuals;
- a reopen handle for stronger observers;
- the venture ledger segment that produced it; and
- a composition or substitution rule, if separately checked.

The effective grammar then changes by

\[
G_{t+1}
=
G_t[\sigma].
\]

This notation means that later proposal generation may invoke \(\sigma\) as a
word. It does not make \(\sigma\) a stable Rust operation, an equation cell, a
program identity, or an irreducible metaphysical primitive.

The decisive feedback test is:

> Can a later internally generated candidate use the promoted character in a
> way that was shorter, newly reachable under the scheduler, or outside the
> former certified scope?

If not, promotion is only archival naming and has not yet altered effective
imagination.

## 9. Abstraction and forgetting after a venture

A successful characteristic necessarily hides detail. The right response is
not to forbid abstraction, but to separate public compression from historical
erasure.

Let

\[
P
\xrightarrow{\chi_Q}
N
\]

be the public characteristic. Then a promoted word should retain a fibre or
sidecar \(R\) containing at least those process distinctions needed for:

- provenance audit;
- scope checking;
- stronger-observer reopening;
- cost reconstruction;
- counterexample analysis; and
- future recombination.

Closing a Failure aperture may internalize the selected thread. It does not
authorize deleting the unsuccessful threads, their costs, or the fact that the
selection was observer-relative.

A future right-to-forget judgment would need to state:

1. the observer and purpose;
2. which distinctions are being erased;
3. why no declared future obligation depends on them;
4. whether an external audit copy remains; and
5. what reopening becomes impossible.

No such judgment exists in the current project.

## 10. Relationship to the universal lift

The universal-lift proposal of 0080 gives a geometric image for potential
unbounded reuse:

\[
\text{finite sealed word downstairs}
\longrightarrow
\text{arbitrarily long finite lifts upstairs}.
\]

The present ledger supplies the missing operational condition:

\[
\boxed{
\text{a lift is a breakthrough only when the next segment was internally
generated, finitely tried, and historically charged}.
}
\]

An infinite Cayley graph, prefix tree, or hyperbolic cover alone does not make
an observer imaginative. A fixed external user could choose every new path.
Endogeneity resides in the finite proposal transition and its feedback into
\(G,S,A,H,\Lambda\), not in the cardinality of the ambient space.

Likewise, geometric distance is not yet venture cost. The current three-domain
cost vector may later be compared with a path metric, holonomy, curvature, or
physical energy, but none of those identifications is presently established.

## 11. Correction to the learning--proof duality

The earlier proposal reads learning and proof through a shared characteristic
kernel. The correction adds a third motion.

\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{learning}
&:\text{ evidence and trials reshape the completion and vocabulary},\\
\text{proof}
&:\text{ a finite certificate establishes invariance in a declared scope},\\
\text{imagination}
&:\text{ an endogenous generator proposes a new scope obligation}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Proof can close a feature on \(S_t\). Learning can use experience to refine
\(S_t\) and \(A_t\). Imagination can propose \(c\notin S_t\).

The same normalization or characteristic algorithm may participate in both
learning and proof, but it does not generate its own next domain merely by
returning a normal form. A self-duality theorem would need to include the
contravariant relation between proposal, counterexample, residual, and
certificate, not only equality of the final characteristic.

A more complete cycle is

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Failure}
\to
\operatorname{Imagine}
\to
\operatorname{Trial}
\to
\operatorname{Ledger}
\to
\operatorname{Characteristic}
\to
\operatorname{Vocabulary}
\to
\operatorname{Failure}'.
}
\]

This cycle has no privileged final closure.

## 12. Minimal breakthrough judgment

A finite run may report a scoped breakthrough only if all of the following
hold:

1. **Endogenous origin:** the candidate is produced by the declared \(J\)
   machinery from the prior observer state, not selected from a hidden answer
   table or injected as the expected test result.
2. **Scope crossing:** a machine-checkable record shows that the candidate was
   outside the previous characteristic certificate.
3. **Admissibility:** the candidate has a legal typed boundary in the declared
   ambient constructor grammar.
4. **Finite trial:** the candidate receives a bounded execution and checking
   attempt.
5. **Complete ledger:** generation, budget, actual cost, trace, outcome, and
   residual are retained.
6. **Typed outcome:** failure, unknown, nonrepresentability, and exhaustion are
   not collapsed.
7. **Feedback:** the outcome changes a frontier, constraint, scope,
   scheduling decision, or vocabulary.
8. **Promotion discipline:** any new character is scoped, residual-preserving,
   reopenable, and independently checked.
9. **Effective reuse:** a later proposal can actually use the promoted word.
10. **No fixed global depth:** the generator and fair scheduler are defined
    for arbitrary finite continuation, although each validation run remains
    bounded.

Items 1 through 8 establish one local breakthrough. Items 9 and 10 test
whether the mechanism supports open-ended vocabulary rather than one isolated
novelty.

## 13. Red-team boundary

The proposal fails as a breakthrough account if:

1. every candidate is drawn from a pre-enumerated finite fixture table;
2. an external caller supplies all out-of-scope combinations;
3. novelty means only an unseen sample inside the old scope;
4. random syntax is counted without typed origin or admissibility;
5. failures and timeouts disappear from the record;
6. a scalar reward replaces the causal cost and residual history;
7. `NotRepresentable` is treated as false;
8. timeouts authorize negative pruning;
9. one successful instance is promoted to a universal schema;
10. promotion erases source, occurrence, copy, schedule, or trial history;
11. the promoted character is never used by later generation;
12. a finite tested depth is reported as an infinite execution;
13. fair enumeration is reported as discovery of every meaningful concept;
14. host-language search or normalization supplies hidden semantic authority;
15. the universal cover is invoked as a substitute for a generator; or
16. the system claims an absolute escape from all formal boundaries.

Institutional stability is also a risk. A successful character can reduce
future cost while biasing the scheduler away from alternatives. Therefore
reopening, retained counterexamples, and an explicit exploration budget are
part of open-endedness, not optional debugging aids.

## 15. Conservative conclusion

A finite observer does not break finitude by learning one stable pattern.
Nor does it do so by possessing an infinite ambient space in a diagram.

The proposed operational meaning is:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\text{breakthrough of present finitude}
={}&
\text{endogenous out-of-scope proposal}\\
&+\text{finite typed trial}\\
&+\text{structured cost and failure history}\\
&+\text{feedback into future vocabulary}.
\end{aligned}
}
\]

Every event remains finite. What is open is the sequence of possible
vocabulary revisions, not a completed infinity stored by the observer.

This is still a proposal. Its value is that it can fail concretely: the next
calibration can reveal that candidate generation is external, that scope
crossing is only nominal, that cost is not preserved, that promotion is
unsound, or that the new word never changes what the observer can generate.
