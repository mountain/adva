## 1. What this adds to the 2026-09-10 record

[The lattice-polar record](lattice-polar-and-mirror-boundary.md) proved a
negative admission theorem for one fixed centered polytope by exhibiting a
single nonintegral pairing, and argued analytically that neither a change of
linear coordinates nor a uniform scale repairs it. This record adds four
things and changes none of that result:

1. an **iff criterion** — the minimal-lattice lemma — that decides the
   existence question by one finite pairing check over two vertex lists;
2. the **minimal witness lattice** `M = Z-span(Vert(P))` and its index in
   `Z^d`, so a positive verdict arrives with a lattice rather than with prose;
3. the **facet-normal reading** of the TO24 failure as the denominator pair
   `(2, 3)`, hence as a scale-invariant obstruction rather than an accident of
   the declared coordinates;
4. instances in **two and three dimensions** with positive controls that are
   not cubes, so the criterion is exercised on both outcomes in both
   dimensions.

The earlier certificate is re-verified inside this run rather than quoted; the
replay section below reports that check separately.

## 2. The minimal-lattice lemma

Let `P` be a full-dimensional polytope in `Q^d` with the origin in its
interior, `P^o = {y : dot(x,y) <= 1 for every x in P}` its polar under the
declared pairing, and `Vert(P)`, `Vert(P^o)` the two vertex sets.

> **Lemma.** There exists a full-rank lattice `M` in `Q^d` with
> `Vert(P) subset M` and `Vert(P^o) subset M*` **if and only if** every pairing
> `dot(v, y)` with `v` in `Vert(P)` and `y` in `Vert(P^o)` is an integer. When
> this holds, `M = Z-span(Vert(P))` is a witness.

*Proof.* (⇐) Let `M = Z-span(Vert(P))`. It is full rank because `P` is full
dimensional. Every `y` in `Vert(P^o)` pairs integrally with every generator of
`M`, hence with every element of `M` by linearity, so `y` lies in
`M* = Hom(M, Z)`. (⇒) Suppose `M` witnesses the condition. Since `M` contains
`Vert(P)` it contains the lattice those points generate, so
`Z-span(Vert(P))` is a sublattice of `M`; duality reverses inclusion, so
`M*` is a sublattice of `Z-span(Vert(P))*`. Then `Vert(P^o) subset M*` forces
every pairing with `Vert(P)` to be an integer. ∎

Two consequences are used below. First, the existence question is **decided by
a finite pairing check** and no lattice is searched: the universal quantifier
runs over all lattices through the inclusion argument, not through
enumeration. Second, the same argument shows that **a larger lattice is never
easier**: any candidate can be replaced by the minimal one, so a single
nonintegral pairing excludes every compatible lattice at once. That is exactly
the shape of the 2026-09-10 obstruction, here derived instead of asserted.

For a bounded lattice polytope whose facets are written in the offset-one
normalization `dot(n_F, x) <= 1`, the polar vertices are precisely the facet
normals `n_F`. The criterion therefore reads: *every facet normal must lie in
the dual of the lattice generated by the vertices.*

Notes on the positive rows, because they are what makes the criterion
informative rather than permissive:

- the square's vertices generate the index-two checkerboard lattice, not `Z^2`,
  and its polar vertices still pair integrally — a positive case whose minimal
  lattice is *not* the standard one;
- the cube's vertices generate an index-four sublattice of `Z^3` and the
  octahedral polar vertices pair integrally with it;
- `reflexive_triangle_2d` is the lattice triangle with vertices `(1,0)`,
  `(0,1)`, `(-1,-1)`; its polar vertices are `(1,1)`, `(-2,1)`, `(1,-2)`, and
  all nine pairings between the two vertex sets are integers. Its polar is
  *not* this triangle up to a unimodular change of basis — the two have areas
  `3/2` and `9/2`, so no such equivalence is claimed — which is why the
  criterion, not a shape coincidence, is what the fixture exercises;
- `reflexive_simplex_3d` has all four facet normals equal to polar vertices at
  offset one, which is the offset-one reading in its cleanest form.

The three failing rows report the same denominators. For `to24_3d` the
distinct nonintegral pairing values are exactly `{1/2, -1/2, 1/3, -1/3}`: the
two denominators are the whole obstruction.

## 4. Why the fixed centered TO24 fails

Write the instance as `P = {|x_i| <= a, |x_1|+|x_2|+|x_3| <= b}` with `a = 2`,
`b = 3`. Its polar vertices are the six `(1/a)(+-e_i)` and the eight
`(1/b)(+-1,+-1,+-1)`, which is the 14-vertex set of the 2026-09-10 record.

- `Vert(P)` generates `Z^3` (executed: index 1), so every admissible lattice
  satisfies `M` containing `Z^3`, hence `M*` contained in `Z^3`.
- Reflexivity at offset one therefore demands `1/a` and `1/b` to be integers.
  Neither is.
- A uniform scale multiplies both `a` and `b` and leaves the ratio `a/b = 2/3`
  untouched, so no normalization reaches `1` and `1` together. The scaled
  instance `twice_to24_3d` shows the same obstruction with denominators `4`
  and `6`.
- A unimodular change of coordinates transports the polar contragrediently and
  preserves the denominators; the executed `unimodular_image_of_to24_3d` row
  fails with the same value set, and applying `A` instead of `A^(-T)` to the
  polar is refused by the control below.

The earlier certificate `dot((1,2,0), (1/2,0,0)) = 1/2` is re-computed in this
run as its own check (`documented_pairing_recheck`) together with the
membership of both points in the two vertex sets, so the new criterion and the
old witness are tied together by execution rather than by citation.

The refusal rows are the reason a green run here is a statement about
verification rather than about progress: each of them is a way the criterion
could have been satisfied by accident, and each is recorded as a refusal.

## 7. Boundaries

- The criterion decides the question **for the declared vertex data, pairing
  and origin**. It is not an untrusted-certificate validator, and it says
  nothing about a translated origin, a different shape, another dimension or
  another combinatorial type.
- `NoCompatibleLattice` is a statement about lattice pairs, not a defect of the
  polytope: the same combinatorial type may admit a reflexive realization
  elsewhere, which this record neither searches for nor excludes.
- Python `Fraction` arithmetic is an external oracle here, not Rust semantic
  authority. No `Seal`, no native term, no stable operation and no geometry
  catalog admission follows. The Pascal-rooted growth obligation stays Open,
  and the version-zero `lattice-polar` terminology entry is unchanged: this
  record exercises its check rather than extending its vocabulary.
- No toric variety, no Calabi-Yau hypersurface, no crepant resolution, no
  mirror pair and no Ricci-flat metric is constructed, and no physical reading
  is attached to any pairing.

## 8. Next smallest step

The check is cheap and the criterion is general, so the natural continuation is
not a bigger computation but a sharper question: **bind one geometric frame to
a checked Adva diagram** and say what the external lattice judgment means for
that frame, which is the obligation the 2026-09-10 record already left open. A
second, separate option is to decide the gate for a *declared family* of
Goldberg refinements — but the criterion consumes a shape, and the earlier
record notes that the numerical refinement `m = n = 2^k` supplies no nested
cell map or metric normalization, so that option needs a shape declaration
first. Neither option is authorized or begun here.
