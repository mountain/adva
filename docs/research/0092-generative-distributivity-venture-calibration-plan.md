# Generative Distributivity Venture Calibration Plan

Status: falsifiable engineering plan following
[0091](0091-endogenous-scope-breakthrough-and-venture-ledger.md). This note
does not implement the proposed carrier, change the dependency order in
`RESEARCH_ENGINEERING_AGENDA.md`, or authorize a stable learner, generator,
historical-character identity, logic, energy model, or universal computation
claim.

The purpose is to upgrade the existing distributivity calibration from:

\[
\text{reuse one certified finite pattern}
\]

to the harder question:

\[
\text{can a finite observer generate and audit its own next scope extension?}
\]

Every trial remains finite. The generator may have no fixed global semantic
depth, but all reported evidence is bounded.

---

## 0. Exact question

Starting from the existing research-only `HistoricalCharacterV0` for

\[
a(x+y)
\quad\leadsto\quad
ax+ay,
\]

can one bounded research machine:

1. detect that a requested nested use lies outside the seed character's exact
   certified scope;
2. derive a new typed candidate from the seed character and a witnessed
   Failure aperture, rather than select it from an answer table;
3. retain multiple legal occurrence sites and schedules;
4. give every trial finite fuel under a fair scheduler;
5. send every generated program through the existing Rust-backed checks;
6. record construction, space, and time costs for successful, failed, open,
   and exhausted trials;
7. prove only the declared finite characteristic;
8. promote one successful nested construction as a scoped, residual-preserving
   historical word;
9. use that promoted word to generate a later candidate; and
10. reopen every polynomial close under an occurrence-sensitive observer?

The experiment is successful only if the generated candidate is demonstrably
outside the former certificate and the later candidate demonstrably depends
on the promoted word.

`CertificateObstruction`, or `Open`. It may not issue an all-fillings
`FeatureClosed` result or a promoted aperture character until the relevant

- Rust remains the sole authority for program syntax, types, boundaries,

- A generated source string has no semantic authority until it passes the
  existing Rust-backed boundary.
- A research promotion record is not a stable Rust operation, equation cell,
  rewrite, or program identity.

## 2. Seed scope

Let \(\sigma_1\) denote the present historical distributivity character.

Its actual finite instantiator supports the existing template under declared
variable permutations and exact integer multiplicative contexts. It does not
currently certify arbitrary new internal additive sites or the composition of
multiple distributivity occurrences.

Define the seed certified scope explicitly as \(S_1\). Membership in \(S_1\)
must be decided from the stored scope record, not inferred from successful
normalization.

Examples:

| request | classification before the venture |
|---|---|
| fresh renaming \(b(u+v)\) in a declared existing context | inside \(S_1\), empirical reuse |
| another supported exact integer outer context | inside \(S_1\), empirical reuse |
| \(a(b(x+y)+z)\) with two active distributivity sites | outside \(S_1\), candidate scope venture |
| an expression using an unsupported operation such as \(\exp\) | outside the ambient V0 candidate grammar |
| a wrong-typed hole filling | inadmissible |

This table prevents an unseen sample from being mislabeled as a breakthrough.

The experiment should preserve a machine-readable crossing witness

\[
\operatorname{CrossingCert}
=
\left(
\operatorname{scope\_id},
\operatorname{candidate\_id},
\operatorname{failed\_scope\_predicate},
\operatorname{new\_obligations}
\right).
\]

The crossing certificate proves only nonmembership in the former declared
scope. It does not prove semantic novelty or correctness.

## 3. First out-of-scope fixture

Use the nested expression

\[
P_2
=
a\bigl(b(x+y)+z\bigr).
\]

Its polynomial characteristic is expected to be

\[
N_2
=
abx+aby+az.
\]

The important fact is not that this identity is difficult algebra. It is that
\(P_2\) contains two eligible distributivity sites and therefore admits
different legal local schedules.

One schedule is inner first:

\[
a\bigl(b(x+y)+z\bigr)
\leadsto
a\bigl((bx+by)+z\bigr)
\leadsto
abx+aby+az.
\]

Another is outer first:

\[
a\bigl(b(x+y)+z\bigr)
\leadsto
ab(x+y)+az
\leadsto
abx+aby+az.
\]

Parenthesization and flattening in the displayed polynomial are observer
presentations. The checked program traces must retain the exact actual
operations, copies, sources, occurrences, and event order.

The required observer relation is

\[
Q_{\mathrm{poly}}
\preceq
Q_{\mathrm{occ}},
\]

where:

- \(Q_{\mathrm{poly}}\) may see a common exact polynomial characteristic;
- \(Q_{\mathrm{occ}}\) sees the construction sites, explicit copy behavior,
  occurrence identities, schedule, and history.

The experiment must not construct an equation cell between the two schedules.

## 4. Candidate grammar

The generator needs a finite declared metalanguage but must not contain a
finite list of expected candidates.

A minimal research grammar is:

~~~text
Seed        := HistoricalCharacterRef
Instance    := Instantiate(Seed, TypedRenaming, ExactContext)
Candidate   := Instance
             | Plug(typed_hole, Candidate, fresh_typed_term)
             | Compose(site_left, site_right, Candidate)
             | Reobserve(observer, Candidate)
~~~

The exact constructors may change after implementation pressure. Their
obligations may not.

### 4.1 `Instantiate`

Reuse an existing character inside its stored scope. This is baseline
generalization, not scope breakthrough.

### 4.2 `Plug`

Place a checked instance into a fresh typed context derived from an open
boundary or failure aperture. The resulting program may leave the former
scope.

### 4.3 `Compose`

Select two retained eligible occurrence sites and form a finite composition
obligation. It does not assume that local certificates compose.

### 4.4 `Reobserve`

Run a stronger declared observer over the same checked artifacts. It may reopen
a characteristic but does not transform the program.

The generator should produce \(P_2\) structurally by plugging one
\(\sigma_1\) instance into a fresh outer distributivity context. The fixture
may declare the seed types and fresh symbol supply, but it may not store
`P_2 -> N_2` as the generator's answer.

## 7. Exact finite cost record

Every trial receives a declared budget

\[
\mathbf B_i
=
\left(
B_i^K,
B_i^X,
B_i^t
\right)
\]

and returns actual consumption

\[
\mathbf C_i
=
\left(
C_i^K,
C_i^X,
C_i^t
\right).
\]

The first implementation should use exact nonnegative integers.

### 7.1 Construction cost \(C^K\)

Record separately where possible:

- candidate-grammar constructor count;
- generated source size and AST node count;
- fresh source and occurrence obligations;
- number and size of compiled checked artifacts;
- characteristic and certificate obligations;
- promoted-word representation size.

### 7.2 Space cost \(C^X\)

Record:

- number of simultaneously live candidate states;
- number of retained occurrence sites;
- number of legal schedules or fillings represented;
- maximum frontier width;
- retained residual artifact size;
- peak research-harness storage if measured.

### 7.3 Time cost \(C^t\)

Record:

- generator transitions;
- scheduler advances;
- compilation and checking calls;
- checked program events;
- normalization or characteristic steps;
- fuel consumed by each unresolved obligation.

A cost field must state whether it is:

- derived from an authoritative checked artifact;
- counted by research orchestration;
- measured on one platform; or
- a conservative bound.

The first experiment must not aggregate these values into energy. Platform
energy and wall-clock time may later be attached as measurements, never as
semantic identities.

Two schedules with equal cost vectors still retain distinct traces.

## 8. Fair finite scheduling

A finite run can use dovetailing over:

\[
(\text{candidate depth},
\text{site choice},
\text{schedule},
\text{proof obligation}).
\]

At global step \(n\), advance each admitted obligation whose encoded rank and
local step fit the declared dovetail schedule. The exact ranking function and
tie order must be deterministic and stored.

Required properties:

1. every individual advance is finite;
2. no failed or slow candidate blocks all later candidates;
3. timeout leaves the candidate open;
4. increasing the global fuel extends the previous trace rather than
   rewriting it;
5. no fixed semantic maximum depth is stored in the generator; and
6. test configuration still declares a finite maximum fuel and checked depth.

Fairness is relative to the enumerated admissible grammar. It is not a
guarantee that every useful mathematical concept will be proposed.

## 13. Experiment E: vocabulary feedback

Promotion is meaningful only if it changes later generation.

Use \(\sigma_2\) to propose a fresh renamed fixture such as

\[
r\bigl(s(u+v)+w\bigr),
\]

and then a one-level extension such as

\[
c\left(
a\bigl(b(x+y)+z\bigr)+w
\right).
\]

The first checks typed reuse of \(\sigma_2\). The second checks that a generator
can use \(\sigma_2\) as a component in a candidate outside
\(\operatorname{Scope}(\sigma_2)\).

Required evidence:

1. the later generation trace contains an explicit reference to
   \(\sigma_2\);
2. removing \(\sigma_2\) from \(G_t\) changes the derivation or makes the
   candidate unreachable under the same finite budget;
3. macro expansion of \(\sigma_2\) remains available for audit;
4. the resulting program receives fresh checked identities;
5. the new request again starts outside the current scope rather than being
   falsely certified by promotion;
6. all costs include expansion, checking, and residual retention; and
7. a stronger observer reopens the reused characteristic.

This is the decisive vocabulary-change test.

## 14. Experiment F: open-ended finite ladder

Define the structural family recursively:

\[
D_1(a;x,y)
=
a(x+y),
\]

\[
D_{n+1}
=
a_{n}
\left(
D_n+z_n
\right).
\]

The exact variable and type environment must be generated freshly at each
finite \(n\).

For a declared test range, initially \(1\leq n\leq4\):

1. generate \(D_{n+1}\) from a retained character for \(D_n\) and one new
   typed aperture;
2. verify that the new candidate is outside the former exact scope;
3. enumerate or finitely represent the declared schedule alternatives;
4. run under increasing finite fuel;
5. record costs even when the next level exhausts fuel;
6. promote only at levels with the required coverage and invariance evidence;
7. use any promoted level to generate the next; and
8. retain one complete lineage chain from \(\sigma_1\) to the final tested
   candidate.

The generator constructor must not contain `max_depth = 4`. Four is a test
budget, not the semantics.

Passing this ladder demonstrates potentially unbounded finite continuation of
one scoped construction law. It does not establish universal computation,
logical completeness, or discovery of unrelated vocabulary.

## 15. Experiment G: failure changes the future

Configure three deliberately unsuccessful ventures.

### 15.1 Semantic counterexample

Propose an incorrect characteristic such as one that omits a copied factor.
The Rust-backed exact checks should produce a counterexample. The ledger must
add a constraint preventing the same unsupported promotion while retaining
the failed candidate.

### 15.2 Nonrepresentable extension

Insert an unsupported operation or boundary shape. The result should open a
representation or language aperture, not add a semantic falsehood.

### 15.3 Fuel exhaustion

Give one valid nested candidate insufficient fuel. It remains open. A later
run with more fuel must extend its history and may succeed without rewriting
the earlier exhaustion as error.

The scheduler after each case should be observably different because it reads
the updated constraint, aperture, or prior cost. If failures are merely logged
to text and never influence a subsequent proposal or budget decision, the
feedback claim has failed.

## 17. Negative controls

The calibration must fail under each of the following mutations.

| mutation | required detection |
|---|---|
| finite answer table replaces the generator | endogenous-origin failure |
| external caller supplies every candidate | proposal-provenance failure |
| unseen in-scope instance labeled breakthrough | scope-crossing failure |
| random ill-typed syntax counted as imagination | admissibility failure |
| failed trials omitted from ledger | history-completeness failure |
| scalar reward replaces cost vector and trace | cost-carrier failure |
| timeout prunes the candidate | failure-typing violation |
| `NotRepresentable` becomes `Refuted` | result-algebra violation |
| two schedules normalized to one program identity | provenance/identity violation |
| polynomial equality authorizes promotion | coverage-certificate obstruction |
| residual dropped before promotion | reopen obstruction |
| promoted word never used later | vocabulary-feedback failure |
| Python result accepted without Rust checking | authority violation |
| global generator depth fixed to test depth | open-endedness failure |
| sampled numeric agreement replaces exact characteristic | oracle violation |

A harness that passes positive algebraic fixtures while missing one of these
controls has not calibrated breakthrough.

## 19. Acceptance criteria

The first calibration succeeds only if:

1. the seed scope \(S_1\) is explicit and machine checked;
2. an in-scope unseen instance is not mislabeled as breakthrough;
3. \(P_2\) receives a concrete crossing witness;
4. candidate generation starts from the retained Failure aperture;
5. no finite expected-answer table is required;
6. inner-first and outer-first schedules remain distinct checked histories;
7. both schedules yield the same declared exact polynomial characteristic;
8. occurrence reopening returns a concrete process distinction;
9. all generated programs pass the Rust-backed boundary;
10. every trial, including failure and exhaustion, has a structured ledger;
11. cost remains a three-domain vector plus trace;
12. a coverage obstruction prevents premature promotion;
13. a promoted \(\sigma_2\) retains scope, residual, ledger, and reopen handle;
14. a later proposal actually uses \(\sigma_2\);
15. removing \(\sigma_2\) changes finite reachability or derivation cost;
16. one failed venture changes a later constraint, frontier, or schedule;
17. increasing fuel extends rather than falsifies an exhausted trial;
18. the generator has no fixed global semantic-depth field;
19. all tests declare finite budgets; and
20. no stable logic, universal computation, absolute novelty, or energy claim
    is emitted.

## 20. Falsification conditions

The research hypothesis must be revised if implementation shows that:

- scope crossing cannot be represented independently of normalization success;
- the proposed \(J\) operators require the expected target to be supplied by
  the fixture;
- alternative schedules cannot be retained without inventing identities;
- costs cannot be attached without losing causal provenance;
- promotion requires silently treating a pairwise witness as all-fillings
  invariance;
- a promoted word offers no effective generative or compression difference;
- failure feedback causes unsound pruning or permanent lock-in;
- fair scheduling requires a fixed global depth;
- replay cannot distinguish historical attempts with the same candidate text;
  or
- the existing Rust-backed boundary cannot validate the generated finite
  artifacts without an unauthorized semantic extension.

A negative result at any of these points is useful. It identifies whether the
missing component lies in scope representation, generative grammar,
certificate composition, process identity, scheduler semantics, or cost
accounting.

## 21. Proposed implementation order

### Stage 0: preserve the current chain

Keep 0088 unchanged as pairwise reusable evidence. Implement or otherwise
settle 0090 Experiments A--F before reporting all-fillings closure.

### Stage A: research-only scope and ledger carrier

Implement deterministic records for:

- explicit `ScopeV0`;
- `ScopeCrossingV0`;
- typed venture outcomes;
- three-domain budgets and costs;
- complete ordered ledger entries; and
- replayable generation traces.

No promotion is needed to validate this carrier.

### Stage B: bounded generator and schedule experiment

Generate \(P_2\), its occurrence sites, and both schedules from the seed
character and Failure aperture. Reuse existing Rust-backed compilation,
observer, characteristic, and reopen checks.

Return `AcceptedPair` or a more conservative outcome until coverage exists.

### Stage C: finite completion and scoped promotion

After the frontier and coverage carrier passes its own controls, enumerate the
declared finite completion family and test feature invariance. Create
\(\sigma_2\) only if every promotion gate passes.

### Stage D: vocabulary feedback ladder

Use \(\sigma_2\) in fresh and one-level-deeper candidates. Test increasing
finite depths and fuel without installing a global bound in the generator.

### Stage E: promotion decision

Decide, in a separate ADR, whether any surviving part belongs:

- entirely in research Python;
- in a Rust-owned exact scope/certificate carrier;
- inside observer specialization;
- in a future formula language; or
- in a distinct learning and proposal layer.

No answer is selected by this plan.

## 22. Conservative target

The desired first executable result is:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\text{one checked historical character encounters an explicit scope failure;}\\
&\text{the observer generates a typed nested candidate from that failure;}\\
&\text{finite competing trials are checked and charged;}\\
&\text{a scoped successful word retains every residual;}\\
&\text{that word changes the generation of the next finite candidate.}
\end{aligned}
}
\]

This would be stronger than finite generalization and weaker than universal
learning. It would supply the first operational evidence that Adva's finite
observer can alter its effective vocabulary through its own audited ventures,
while remaining honest about metalanguage, budgets, failures, and unresolved
world structure.
