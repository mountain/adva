# A Proposal for Logic on a 3-Form

Status: proposal only, following
[`0044-finite-triadic-satisfaction-logic.md`](0044-finite-triadic-satisfaction-logic.md)
and
[`0045-logic-as-learned-characteristic.md`](0045-logic-as-learned-characteristic.md).

This note proposes a research programme.  It does **not** claim that Adva now
has a natural-deduction system, a categorical hyperdoctrine, a new foundation
of logic, or a stable logic API.

The proposal starts from one typed three-domain satisfaction form

\[
\Lambda_Q:
\mathcal T_Q\times\mathcal X_Q\times\mathcal K_Q
\longrightarrow
\mathbb S.
\]

The intended readings are:

- \(\mathcal T_Q\): finite temporal histories, searches, or normalization
  traces visible to observer \(Q\);
- \(\mathcal X_Q\): finite spatial states, loci, or extents visible to
  \(Q\);
- \(\mathcal K_Q\): finite constructions, witnesses, or programs available to
  \(Q\); and
- \(\mathbb S\): positive observable evidence, without automatic Boolean
  complement.

The proposal is that the **sections** of \(\Lambda_Q\), not the full powerset
of its carrier, generate the first admissible propositions.  Their refinement
order supplies semantic entailment.  Observer maps supply substitution and,
only where adjoints exist, candidate quantifiers.  Construction and temporal
coordinates may later refine semantic entailment into proof-relevant
derivation.

This note is deliberately stronger about questions than answers.  Every major
claim is paired with a promotion gate or falsifier.  It changes no stable Adva
semantics, no `claims.toml` entry, and no active engineering priority.

---

## 0. Executive proposal

The candidate structure is

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_Q
\longrightarrow
\text{opposite-pair sections}
\longrightarrow
\text{generated propositions}
\longrightarrow
\text{entailment}
\longrightarrow
\text{observer transport}.
}
\]

Only the first two arrows and a finite support preorder were computed in note
0044.  The remaining arrows are proposed research targets.

The key restraint is:

\[
\mathcal L_Q
\ne
\mathcal P(
\mathcal T_Q\times\mathcal X_Q\times\mathcal K_Q
)
\quad\text{by default}.
\]

An arbitrary finite powerset is already a Boolean algebra.  Calling it a
3-form logic would add no triadic content.  Naturalness can only come from a
restricted family generated by sections and preserved by domain transport,
observer refinement, and singular crossing.

# Part I. Primitive data

## 1. The three-domain relation

For a fixed finite observer \(Q\), let

\[
\Lambda_Q
\subseteq
\mathcal T_Q\times\mathcal X_Q\times\mathcal K_Q
\]

be the positively accepted triples.  The same object may be presented as a
Sierpinski-valued map.

An accepted triple

\[
(t,x,k)\in\Lambda_Q
\]

says only that the declared temporal history, spatial reading, and
construction are compatible positive evidence at observer \(Q\).  It does not
identify the three coordinates and does not make them interchangeable.

The 3-form is therefore a typed compatibility relation before it is a logic.

## 2. Opposite-pair sections as atoms

Fixing two coordinates gives a predicate on the third.

Temporal section:

\[
A^t_{x,k}
=
\{t\in\mathcal T_Q
\mid
\Lambda_Q(t,x,k)=1\}.
\]

Spatial section:

\[
A^X_{t,k}
=
\{x\in\mathcal X_Q
\mid
\Lambda_Q(t,x,k)=1\}.
\]

Constructive section:

\[
A^K_{t,x}
=
\{k\in\mathcal K_Q
\mid
\Lambda_Q(t,x,k)=1\}.
\]

This is the proposed logical reading of the triangle principle: a vertex
obtains its primitive vocabulary from a fixed observation on the opposite
pair.

The set of all such sections is the atomic generator family

\[
\mathcal G_Q
=
\mathcal G_Q^t
\cup
\mathcal G_Q^X
\cup
\mathcal G_Q^K.
\]

The superscripts name sorts.  A temporal atom and a constructive atom are not
subsets of the same carrier and cannot be combined without an explicit
translation or common context.

## 3. Admissible propositions

The proposed proposition family is the least typed family containing the
sections and closed under only those operations justified by the observer:

\[
\mathcal L_Q
=
\langle
\mathcal G_Q
\rangle_{
\text{declared finite operations, reindexing, transport}
}.
\]

At the first stage, finite conjunction and disjunction of same-sort supports
may be tested as intersection and union:

\[
\llbracket A\wedge B\rrbracket_Q
=
\llbracket A\rrbracket_Q
\cap
\llbracket B\rrbracket_Q,
\]

\[
\llbracket A\vee B\rrbracket_Q
=
\llbracket A\rrbracket_Q
\cup
\llbracket B\rrbracket_Q.
\]

Even these operations need a construction interpretation before they become
natural-deduction rules.  Intersection does not by itself build a paired
witness; union does not by itself build a tagged alternative.

Implication should not be inserted merely because the finite ambient powerset
has a Boolean implication.  A candidate implication belongs to
\(\mathcal L_Q\) only if the generated family has the required relative
pseudocomplement or a checked construction-level transformer.

## 4. Semantic entailment

For propositions of the same sort, the first semantic judgment is

\[
A\preceq_Q B
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\llbracket A\rrbracket_Q
\subseteq
\llbracket B\rrbracket_Q.
\]

For finite enumerated supports this is decidable and returns either inclusion
or a counterexample point.  For an open or incompletely searched support, lack
of a counterexample is not a proof.  The result must be `Unknown` unless a
certificate establishes inclusion.

The order is initially a preorder because two differently generated formulas
may have the same finite extent while retaining different syntax,
construction, or future transport.

# Part II. A candidate proof-relevant judgment

## 5. Realization before derivation

A possible atomic realization judgment is

\[
t\triangleright_Q k:A@x.
\]

It reads:

> along temporal trace \(t\), construction \(k\) realizes proposition \(A\)
> at spatial observation \(x\), as accepted by observer \(Q\).

For an atomic proposition generated from \(\Lambda_Q\), this reduces to a
membership check on a typed accepted triple.

The judgment is only notation at this stage.  Note 0044 computes accepted
triples but does not define a derivation datatype, proof equality,
normalization, or substitution.

## 6. Semantic consequence versus proof transport

There are two possible consequence judgments.

The extensional one is support inclusion:

\[
A\models_Q B.
\]

The proof-relevant one would require a construction \(f\) that transports
realizers:

\[
f:
\operatorname{Ev}_Q(A)
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Ev}_Q(B).
\]

A future derivation judgment might be written

\[
\Gamma\vdash_Q^f B,
\]

with a temporal certificate that executing or normalizing \(f\) produces the
declared transport.  The exact syntax is intentionally not fixed here.

Soundness would require

\[
\Gamma\vdash_Q^f B
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Gamma\models_Q B.
\]

Completeness would require a converse relative to a declared fragment and
observer.  No such theorem is currently available.

## 7. Why ordinary natural-deduction rules cannot be copied unchanged

Candidate logical operations expose missing construction semantics.

| rule family | required constructive data | current status |
|---|---|---|
| conjunction introduction | pair of witnesses and compatible trace synchronization | not defined |
| conjunction elimination | checked projections retaining provenance | not defined as logic |
| disjunction introduction | tagged alternatives | finite tagged logical data not stable |
| disjunction elimination | bounded case analysis with branch provenance | not stable |
| implication introduction | hypothetical construction and discharge/binding | outside binder-free PSC0 |
| implication elimination | checked application/substitution | not a logic rule yet |
| existential introduction | witness plus proposition realization | proposed only |
| existential elimination | scope-safe witness use | proposed only |
| contraction | explicit copy construction | cannot be silently assumed |
| weakening | explicit discard or observer irrelevance | cannot be silently assumed |

This table is not a reason to import a larger logic into the Rust core.  It is
a diagnostic list of what a genuine 3-form natural deduction would have to
construct and certify.

## 8. Default non-Boolean posture

The primitive codomain is \(\mathbb S\), so excluded middle and double-negation
elimination are not default rules.

A Boolean fragment may emerge when an observer supplies a decidable
partition.  Its promotion should record:

- a decision procedure or clopen decomposition;
- treatment of incomplete search;
- the observer map that created the binary view; and
- the residual distinctions merged by Booleanization.

Thus Boolean logic may be a valid local fibre of the proposal without being
the universal starting point.

# Part III. Observer-indexed logic

## 9. Reindexing along observer maps

Let \(Q'\) refine \(Q\), with a forgetting map

\[
q:
\mathcal O_{Q'}
\longrightarrow
\mathcal O_Q.
\]

Inverse image gives candidate substitution or reindexing:

\[
q^*:
\mathcal L_Q
\longrightarrow
\mathcal L_{Q'}.
\]

The first naturalness requirement is that \(q^*\) carry generated
propositions to generated propositions, rather than only to arbitrary subsets
of the fine carrier.

If it does not, either the generator family is too small or the observer map
is not a logical morphism for the proposed calculus.

## 10. Candidate quantifiers as adjoints

Where they exist inside the admissible proposition families, quantifiers may
be characterized by

\[
\exists_q\dashv q^*\dashv\forall_q.
\]

The left adjoint forgets by existence of a fine lift.  The right adjoint keeps
only coarse points all of whose fine lifts satisfy the proposition.

The Legendre quotient of note 0044 gives the smallest diagnostic example:

\[
q^{-1}(\ell_{[I]})
=
\{\ell_I,\ell_{-I}\}.
\]

For the exact-identity proposition \(P_I=\{\ell_I\}\):

- existential forgetting can report coarse projective closure because one
  fine lift is \(I\);
- universal forgetting cannot report “exactly \(I\)” at the coarse closed
  point because another fine lift is \(-I\).

This difference is the logical content of the residual fibre.  It disappears
if quotienting is treated as simple deletion.

## 11. A candidate indexed structure

If generated proposition families, reindexing, logical operations, and
adjoints satisfy the appropriate laws, the observer tower begins to resemble
an indexed predicate calculus or hyperdoctrine:

\[
Q
\longmapsto
\mathcal L_Q.
\]

“Resemble” is intentional.  The required categories, products, Beck-Chevalley
conditions, Frobenius laws, proof terms, and coherence certificates have not
been supplied.

The name should not be promoted until at least one nontrivial finite fixture
checks the relevant laws and one counterexample identifies their boundary.

# Part IV. Domain transport and crossing

## 12. Translation between the three sorts

The three section sorts need explicit translations before they can participate
in one calculus:

\[
F_{tX},\qquad F_{XK},\qquad F_{Kt}
\]

or whichever typed directions the finite model actually supports.

A translation should include:

1. a map of proposition generators;
2. a semantic comparison of supports;
3. a construction or certificate where proof relevance is claimed; and
4. a residual for distinctions lost in translation.

The crossing-coherence prism in note 0043 warns that pairwise commutation may
close on a coarse charge while construction history remains unresolved.
Logical translation must not silently erase the same residual.

## 13. Transport around a singular crossing

For a crossing \(c\), each carrier may change:

\[
J_c^t,
\qquad
J_c^X,
\qquad
J_c^K.
\]

The form itself should be transported:

\[
\Lambda_{Q,-}
\rightsquigarrow
\Lambda_{Q,+}.
\]

A candidate logical transport must compare “form a section, then cross” with
“cross the data, then form a section.”  Strict agreement is one possible
result; a typed residual or `Unknown` is equally legitimate.

The proposal is natural only if its generated proposition family is stable
under this transport.  Arbitrary powerset closure would make this test
vacuous.

## 14. Genuine triadicity test

Pairwise projections do not generally determine a ternary relation.  This
supplies a decisive experiment.

Construct two finite forms

\[
\Lambda_Q,
\qquad
\Lambda'_Q
\]

with identical projections onto

\[
\mathcal T_Q\times\mathcal X_Q,
\quad
\mathcal X_Q\times\mathcal K_Q,
\quad
\mathcal K_Q\times\mathcal T_Q,
\]

but different accepted triples.

Then ask:

1. do opposite-pair sections distinguish the two global fillings?
2. does the generated logic retain that distinction after observer transport?
3. can any pairwise logical presentation reconstruct it?

If the proposed calculus cannot distinguish the forms, it has collapsed back
to pairwise data.  If it can, the counterexample provides the first evidence
of irreducibly triadic logical content.

Note 0044 does not pass this test: its diagonal finite fixture may be
reconstructible from its pairwise projections.

# Part V. The unresolved L/R and startup questions

## 15. Contextual order instead of imposed order

The 3-form does not magically make two incomparable left and right programs
globally ordered.  Its possible contribution is to replace an untyped demand

\[
L<R
\]

with a family of typed contextual comparisons.

For an opposite-pair context \(c\), let \(L_c\) and \(R_c\) be the generated
sections associated with the two programs.  A local order is

\[
L\preceq_Q^c R
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{supp}_Q(L_c)
\subseteq
\operatorname{supp}_Q(R_c).
\]

A bounded observer may recover a global order only if every declared context
agrees and the transports between contexts are coherent:

\[
L\preceq_Q R
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\forall c\in\mathcal C_Q,
\ L\preceq_Q^c R.
\]

If some contexts reverse the inclusion or remain unresolved, the correct
result is incomparability or `Unknown`, not a forced `L | R` reduction.

Thus the proposed simplification is not “compute a hidden total order.”  It is
“factor the order question into finite sections and expose the exact contexts
that obstruct coherence.”

## 16. Relation to startup calibration

At startup, the temporal, spatial, and constructive computers may begin with
different local coordinate conventions.  Before their propositions can be
compared, translation maps and a common observer contract must be calibrated.

This resembles the L/R problem:

- L/R asks whether local section orders assemble into a coherent comparison;
- startup asks whether local coordinate readings assemble into a coherent
  common form.

Both may therefore be instances of a broader descent problem: local data plus
comparison maps may or may not admit a global compatible section.

They are not yet proved to be the same problem.  To establish identity one
must construct a translation taking startup calibration defects to L/R order
defects and preserving their residuals.  Until that exists, “same pattern” is
the strongest justified statement.

## 17. A finite test for the shared pattern

A bounded calibration should:

1. choose two incompatible local encodings of the same finite carrier;
2. build temporal, spatial, and constructive translation maps;
3. derive L/R sections before calibration;
4. calibrate the three maps and derive the sections again;
5. determine whether contextual incomparability disappears;
6. retain the transformation between the two comparison reports; and
7. return a proof, counterexample, or `Unknown`.

If calibration always imposes the desired order by definition, the experiment
is circular.  The order must be a consequence of independently checked
compatibility.

# Part VI. What would count as natural deduction

## 18. Minimum formal-system package

The phrase “a natural deduction on a 3-form” should be reserved for a system
with at least:

1. an intrinsic grammar of typed formulas generated from sections;
2. contexts and a derivation judgment;
3. typed introduction and elimination rules;
4. proof objects or certified derivation histories;
5. explicit structural rules for copy, discard, and exchange;
6. substitution and observer reindexing;
7. operational or normalization meaning in the temporal domain;
8. satisfaction meaning in the spatial domain;
9. witness meaning in the constructive domain;
10. a soundness theorem for a declared fragment; and
11. a bounded completeness result or explicit counterexample.

The current finite satisfaction computation supplies items adjacent to 1 and
8, plus a semantic preorder.  It does not supply the package.

## 19. Candidate cyclic interpretation

The long-term hypothesis is a cyclic division of labour:

| domain | possible logical role | missing certificate |
|---|---|---|
| construction \(K\) | proof term, witness, formula construction | typing and substitution |
| time \(T\) | execution, proof search, normalization trace | termination or honest `Unknown` |
| space \(X\) | extent, locus, satisfaction region | observer-relative semantics |

No domain is proposed as the sole foundation.  Fixing the opposite pair gives
the vocabulary with which the third role is observed.

The cycle is not automatically symmetric.  Construction may retain history
that spatial extent forgets, and temporal search may fail to terminate even
when semantic truth is classically determined.  The residuals are part of the
proposed structure, not imperfections to be erased.

## 20. Cut, normalization, and coherence

A genuine proof calculus must distinguish:

- composing proof transports;
- executing the composite through time;
- comparing the resulting spatial extent; and
- retaining construction history across the comparison.

A future cut-elimination or normalization claim would therefore need a
three-domain coherence diagram, not only equality of final truth values.

The weakest acceptable result is observer-relative:

\[
\text{same observed extent}
+
\text{explicit temporal/construction residual}.
\]

Exact proof identity is a stronger claim and must not be inferred from that
shadow.

# Part VII. Research plan and falsifiers

## 21. Staged programme

### Stage A: generated finite propositional fragment

- define atomic formulas only from opposite-pair sections;
- compute the least finite meet/join closure by sort;
- compare it with the ambient powerset;
- require at least one non-generated subset; and
- return exact inclusion witnesses or counterexamples.

### Stage B: ambiguous global fillings

- construct two 3-forms with equal pairwise projections and different triple
  support;
- test whether the generated propositions distinguish them; and
- identify the minimum observer that sees the distinction.

### Stage C: observer transport

- implement reindexing along one exact finite quotient;
- test closure of generated propositions under pullback;
- compute candidate existential and universal adjoints;
- check adjunction laws exhaustively; and
- preserve exact quotient fibres as residuals.

### Stage D: proof-relevant fragment

- choose a bounded conjunction-only or Horn-like fragment;
- define Rust-authoritative proof constructors;
- interpret construction as witness, time as execution, and space as extent;
- prove finite soundness; and
- report whether semantic inclusion without proof transport occurs.

### Stage E: crossing and L/R calibration

- transport generated formulas across one Legendre crossing;
- compute contextual L/R orders;
- compare them before and after three-computer startup calibration; and
- decide whether the two obstruction reports are related by an exact map.

Later stages depend on earlier ones.  None authorizes a stable logic API.

## 22. Falsifiers

The proposal loses its intended content if any of the following persists.

1. Section generation always yields the entire powerset for nontrivial
   fixtures.
2. All purported triadic distinctions are reconstructible from pairwise
   projections.
3. Observer pullback sends generated propositions outside every natural
   generated closure.
4. Candidate quantifiers exist only in the ambient powerset and not in the
   admissible fragment.
5. Support inclusion systematically lacks any construction-level transport.
6. Temporal normalization adds no information beyond a Boolean satisfaction
   check.
7. Construction residuals never affect any later typed continuation.
8. L/R order appears only because calibration defines it rather than because
   compatibility entails it.

A negative result at any stage is useful.  It narrows the correct logical
structure or shows that the 3-form is a presentation of an existing
single-domain calculus rather than a new one.

## 23. Explicit nonclaims

This note does not establish:

- a three-valued logic;
- three independent copies of ordinary logic;
- a natural-deduction formal system;
- implication, negation, or quantifiers in the generated fragment;
- cut elimination, normalization, soundness, or completeness;
- a genuine irreducible triadic invariant;
- a solution to the global L/R reduction problem;
- identity of the L/R and startup calibration problems;
- a stable relation to Chaitin \(\Omega\);
- a universal finite representation of an open world; or
- any stable Adva semantic or implementation API.

## 24. Conservative conclusion

The proposal can be stated in one sentence:

> Start with a Sierpinski-valued compatibility form on time, space, and
> construction; generate typed atomic propositions from its opposite-pair
> sections; order them by observable refinement; and admit logical operations,
> quantifiers, and proof rules only when they survive observer and crossing
> transport with explicit residuals.

This is enough to specify the next experiments.  It is not yet enough to name
a new logic.
