## Mingli's question and the proposed proposition

On 2026-09-06 Mingli Yuan asked how the repository's resources can keep tools
runnable while preserving finite arithmetic truth at the boundary. He proposed
that three related objects must meet before a boundary can be free, and offered
the working proposition: "Differences in phenomena give rise to knowledge."

We preserve that proposition as a research direction rather than importing it
as an axiom. This step tests a conditional operational reading: a checked
distinction becomes a finite knowledge update when it removes a previously
admissible candidate and the changed state actually changes the next choice.
An already known distinction need not yield new knowledge. Noise, observer
drift, unsupported scope transfer, and an incomplete candidate universe also
prevent an unconditional inference from difference to knowledge.

The phenomena in this experiment are declared arithmetic observations, not
new physical measurements. No theorem about all knowledge, human awareness,
physics or an open world follows. Arithmetic-universality and hypothesized-
arithmetic-truth remain Proposed; Research 0090/0092 promotion and the paused
unknown-syntax-building inquiry keep their existing boundaries.

## Conditional finite preservation argument

Fix a point question Q, a finite declared hypothesis universe U, an active
set H contained in U, and an exact checked observation y. Each h has a declared
prediction pred(h,Q). Define

```
H_new = {h in H : pred(h,Q) = y}.
```

If the correct candidate h* belongs to H and the verified observation agrees
with pred(h*,Q), then h* belongs to H_new: its defining equality passes the
filter. Consequently a succession of accepted updates preserves h* by
induction, provided each observation retains those assumptions and scope.
This is an elementary finite filtering invariant, not a proof of Python,
the underlying hardware, all learning, or infinite execution.

The result is NewConstraint only when H_new is a proper nonempty subset of H.
If H_new equals H, the result is NoNewConstraint. If it is empty, report
ModelGap and keep the active state rather than treating an empty model space
as knowledge or free completion. A malformed or inapplicable observation,
failed declared guard, unavailable capability or exhausted fuel must not add
an arithmetic commitment. Checking may still consume resources.

The complete universe is exactly the two predictions declared for this task.
Its enumeration can be checked; this is not coverage of every possible model
of an open world. In particular, it is not the full Research 0090 frontier
coverage theorem.

## What free can mean in this experiment

The positive external outcome is `TaskBoundarySatisfied`: it permits only
the declared external learn/checkpoint action. Its optional `free_candidate`
reading is a task-relative interpretation proposed for Mingli's review.
Native free remains Unimplemented/Proposed, independently of this outcome.

This condition does not remove the existing A/M/nonzero obligations from a
task that requests native execution. Requesting native free is an explicit
negative case here; the Python checker cannot supply the missing adapter or
native witnesses. Neither task admission nor a native arithmetic Seal would
establish a person's consent or real-world acceptance. Those are not required
by this narrowly authorized arithmetic checkpoint, and none is invented.

Resource consistency is local to a submitted history. Copying a valid old
record can still fork an account without an external monotonic service.
The tool neither claims global replay prevention nor refills fuel through a
rename. Subject display-name changes preserve interpretation; the method
itself remains pinned to its exact version.

## Frozen calibration

The main input reuses the checked k27 point receipt from Research 0142. The
two initially active predictions are unit and dyadic-product. They are
supplied as calibration candidates; the correct prediction is not discovered
by a new search or invented syntax. The expected update excludes unit and
changes the next proposed candidate to dyadic-product.

A repeated invocation on the updated singleton should report NoNewConstraint
and retain consumed fuel/history. A fresh k30 product reuses the method with
a newly constructed and checked receipt. No whole 0141/0142 suite is rerun.
An external nonzero-trace guard is required by this method; the zero case
tests that requirement, not a general prohibition on learning about zero.

All seven incomplete presence subsets of the three inputs are tested. Seven
additional complete-triple cases carry stale scope, forged evidence, missing
coverage, native-free demand, zero fuel, a violated nonzero guard, and an
empty survivor set. A subject display rename is the final control. There are
exactly eighteen declared trials including main, repeat and fresh reuse.

The comparison uses the same trials for a presence-only gate and the full
obligation gate. False admission here means permission unsupported by the
declared contract, not necessarily a false mathematical proposition. The
next-choice comparison establishes use of the retained constraint, not a
speedup or an algorithmic improvement over ordinary finite filtering.

One invocation completed with all eighteen expected outcomes. No implementation
repair or repeated suite was needed. The frozen
[contract](0143-knowledge-boundary-contract.json), complete
[report](0143-evidence/report.json), [host cost](0143-evidence/host-cost.json),
[stdout](0143-evidence/stdout.txt), [stderr](0143-evidence/stderr.txt), and

For k = 27, the exact product is
`(1 + 2^-k) * (1 - 2^-k) = 1 - 2^-54`, not one. The checked receipt therefore
excludes the supplied unit prediction. The finite next-candidate selector
changes from unit to dyadic-product. At k = 30, a newly constructed receipt
reuses the same method and retains `1 - 2^-60`. Both correct predictions were
supplied before checking; this experiment does not discover their formula.

| Cases | Count | Actual outcome |
|---|---:|---|
| Main k27 and fresh k30 | 2 | TaskBoundarySatisfied; 2 to 1 candidates; NewConstraint |
| Repeated evidence and subject display rename | 2 | TaskBoundarySatisfied; NoNewConstraint; fuel and history retained |
| Incomplete presence subsets | 7 | Blocked; no arithmetic update |
| Stale scope, forged receipt, missing coverage, native-free demand, zero guard | 5 | Blocked; no arithmetic update |

| All predictions contradicted | 1 | ModelGap; no arithmetic update or action permission |

On these same cases, the presence-only gate grants eleven admissions; the
complete gate grants four. The difference is seven unsupported admissions
prevented under this contract. These are not seven false arithmetic theorems.
There are eighteen admission trials, not eighteen successful arithmetic proofs.
The seven missing-role subsets exhaust only presence combinations; the entire
space of malformed inputs or possible knowledge models was not enumerated.

The report reconstructs the main update and forged-receipt refusal from a
serialized role pool and rechecks both within the same global budget. Both
result and history match. The reused point checker employs a different
traversal from its producer; the candidate filter is cross-checked by integer
cross-multiplication. They share the same Python arithmetic and runtime, so
this is not independent-runtime validation or third-party proof.

ModelGap preserves the old candidates only as a historical snapshot for
revision, not as candidates still supported by the new observation. Its
`next_proposal_after` field records order and confers no permission. The positive
selector results are computed, but have not been used to execute a later native
task or a real-world decision. Initial hypothesis membership and imported
history are conditional inputs, not authenticated discoveries.

verified difference, applicability, actual candidate reduction, repeated
knowledge, model inadequacy and permission to continue. It may help form a
concrete decision question for Jiamin, but no customer task or user benefit was
