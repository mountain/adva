# Finite Context-Generated Proposition Algebra

Status: exploratory finite calibration following
[`0050-finite-triadic-residual-activation.md`](0050-finite-triadic-residual-activation.md).

The executable fixture is
[`tests/python/test_triadic_context_proposition_algebra.py`](../../tests/python/test_triadic_context_proposition_algebra.py).

The preceding activation experiment found one future context that turns a
constructive residual into a spatial proposition.  This note closes the next
finite question exactly:

> What is the complete family of propositions generated by every admitted
> finite context, and what does “complete” mean for a finite observer that
> still forgets residual structure?

The answer has three layers that must not be collapsed.

1. The three admitted programs generate an exact **sixteen-element
   transformation monoid**.  The closure is complete; no word-length bound or
   `Unknown` tail remains.
2. Pulling back one spatial atom through all sixteen transformations produces
   only **five direct context propositions**.  This family is not a Boolean
   algebra.
3. The Boolean algebra generated by those five propositions has **256
   elements**.  It is the complete powerset of the eight contextual
   equivalence classes, but not the powerset of the sixteen fine states.

Thus finite completeness is relative:

\[
\operatorname{Bool}(\mathcal L_X^{\mathrm{ctx}})
\cong
\mathcal P(\widetilde S/{\sim_{\mathrm{ctx}}}),
\]

not

\[
\operatorname{Bool}(\mathcal L_X^{\mathrm{ctx}})
=
\mathcal P(\widetilde S).
\]

This supplies a concrete finite form of the representation principle: a
finite observer can be complete for its declared quotient while remaining
incomplete for the fine or open world.

The host-side state machine, scheduler, transformation monoid, proposition
family, and Boolean envelope are research objects only.  Rust-authoritative
Adva programs check the three scalar coordinate updates.  This note adds no
stable context, scheduler, monoid, proposition, quotient, logic, or
three-computer API; it does not modify `claims.toml`; and it does not change
the active `ProgramSlice` priority.

## 0. Executive construction

Let the triadic core state be

\[
S_0=\{0,1\}^3,
\qquad
s=(t,x,k),
\]

with temporal, spatial, and constructive coordinates.  Attach one audit bit

\[
h\in H=\{0,1\}
\]

to obtain the fine carrier

\[
\widetilde S=S_0\times H.
\]

The audit bit is not a fourth computer.  It is a minimal residual attached to
the checked run, retained so that observer completeness cannot be confused
with equality of fine states.

Use three updates, executed by context words from left to right:

\[
T(t,x,k,h)=(1,x,k,h),
\]

\[
X(t,x,k,h)=(t,t\wedge k,k,h),
\]

and

\[
K(t,x,k,h)=(t,x,1-k,h).
\]

The spatial atom is

\[
A_X=\{(t,x,k,h)\mid x=1\}.
\]

Every context transformation \(m\) generates a proposition by inverse image:

\[
P_m=m^{-1}(A_X).
\]

The five distinct results are

\[
x,
\qquad
t\wedge k,
\qquad
t\wedge\neg k,
\qquad
k,
\qquad
\neg k.
\]

They retain enough joint information to recover \((t,x,k)\), but no context
can read \(h\).  The contextual equivalence classes are therefore exactly

\[
[(t,x,k)]
=
\{(t,x,k,0),(t,x,k,1)\}.
\]

There are eight classes.  Arbitrary Boolean combinations of the five direct
predicates select arbitrary unions of those eight classes, giving \(2^8=256\)
propositions.  The ambient fine powerset has \(2^{16}=65\,536\) propositions.

# Part I. Why this is the next experiment

## 1. From one witness to a closed context space

The previous note used schedule words only up to a declared maximum length.
That was enough to find a positive activation witness, but it could not justify
a negative statement about all future contexts.

The present carrier is finite and every program is a total deterministic
transformation.  Therefore contexts can be quotiented by exact equality of
their induced transformations.  Repeated composition reaches a fixed point:

\[
M=\langle T,X,K\rangle\subseteq\operatorname{End}(S_0).
\]

Once no new transformation appears, every longer word is equal to an already
known transformation.  Exhaustion is a proof of closure for this declared
machine, not a bounded-search guess.

## 2. Why the carrier is closed

The arithmetic activation

\[
x^+=x+tk
\]

from the preceding experiment deliberately allowed \(x\in\mathbf Z\).  It was
suited to signed separation but not to a finite transformation closure.

Here the spatial program overwrites its bit:

\[
x^+=t\wedge k=tk
\]

on binary inputs.  All three updates preserve the Boolean cube.  This is a new
finite calibration, not a claim that overwriting is the canonical spatial law
of a 3-form computer.

## 3. Why the audit bit remains

If the carrier were only \(S_0\), the five predicates would separate every
state.  Their Boolean closure would then be the entire powerset of the cube.
That result is correct but would hide the central observer issue.

The audit bit imports the inert residual boundary already established in the
activation experiment.  Programs neither inspect nor modify it.  Consequently
the logic can become complete on the observable core without becoming
complete on the fine carrier.

This distinction is the finite analogue of an open-world warning: adding a
new readable residual or a new observer may refine the quotient and enlarge
the language.

# Part II. Checked coordinate programs

## 4. Rust-authoritative scalar updates

The fixture links three Adva programs:

```text
open-temporal(t) = 1 + 0 * t
write-spatial(t,k) = t * k
flip-construction(k) = 1 + (-k)
```

The first program uses the old temporal input through a zero-scaled
occurrence.  It does not introduce an implicit discard into the linear core.
Its extensional value is nevertheless one.

Rust validates the program graphs, boundaries, uses, occurrences,
evaluations, and histories.  SymPy supplies only independent scalar identities:

\[
\operatorname{open}(t)=1,
\qquad
\operatorname{write}(t,k)=tk,
\qquad
\operatorname{flip}(k)=1-k.
\]

## 5. Host-side research boundary

Python lifts the scalar results to the tuple updates \(T,X,K\), preserves the
audit bit, composes schedule words, and enumerates the finite closure.

That lifting is not a stable Adva state-transition judgment.  In particular,
the fixture does not provide:

- code as data;
- an interpreter or universal machine;
- a certified scheduler;
- a stable context equality;
- a Rust proposition type; or
- a logical proof certificate.

The finite monoid is evidence about the declared research machine only.

# Part III. Exact transformation monoid

## 6. Composition convention

A word is executed from left to right.  Thus

\[
TX=X\circ T:
\quad
(t,x,k)\longmapsto(1,k,k).
\]

Concatenation agrees with transformation composition:

\[
\llbracket uv\rrbracket
=
\llbracket v\rrbracket\circ\llbracket u\rrbracket.
\]

The empty word \(\varepsilon\) denotes the identity.

## 7. Closure algorithm

Begin with the identity and repeatedly append each generator:

1. put \(\operatorname{id}\) in a queue;
2. for each discovered transformation \(m\), compute \(T\circ m\),
   \(X\circ m\), and \(K\circ m\) under the left-to-right convention;
3. retain a new transformation only if its complete eight-state table has not
   appeared before;
4. stop when the queue is empty.

The procedure terminates with exactly sixteen transformations.  Every pairwise
composition of those sixteen transformations is again in the set.  The
longest selected shortest representative has length four.

## 8. The sixteen normal representatives

The following table gives one breadth-first shortest representative for every
transformation.  It is a computed normal-form table, not a proposed complete
abstract presentation of the monoid.

| word | \(t'\) | \(x'\) | \(k'\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| \(\varepsilon\) | \(t\) | \(x\) | \(k\) |
| \(K\) | \(t\) | \(x\) | \(\neg k\) |
| \(T\) | \(1\) | \(x\) | \(k\) |
| \(X\) | \(t\) | \(t\wedge k\) | \(k\) |
| \(KX\) | \(t\) | \(t\wedge\neg k\) | \(\neg k\) |
| \(TK\) | \(1\) | \(x\) | \(\neg k\) |
| \(TX\) | \(1\) | \(k\) | \(k\) |
| \(XK\) | \(t\) | \(t\wedge k\) | \(\neg k\) |
| \(XT\) | \(1\) | \(t\wedge k\) | \(k\) |
| \(KXK\) | \(t\) | \(t\wedge\neg k\) | \(k\) |
| \(KXT\) | \(1\) | \(t\wedge\neg k\) | \(\neg k\) |
| \(TKX\) | \(1\) | \(\neg k\) | \(\neg k\) |
| \(TXK\) | \(1\) | \(k\) | \(\neg k\) |
| \(XTK\) | \(1\) | \(t\wedge k\) | \(\neg k\) |
| \(KXTK\) | \(1\) | \(t\wedge\neg k\) | \(k\) |
| \(TKXK\) | \(1\) | \(\neg k\) | \(k\) |

## 9. Exact equations and order dependence

The generators satisfy the checked equations

\[
T^2=T,
\qquad
X^2=X,
\qquad
K^2=1.
\]

Temporal opening and constructive flipping commute:

\[
TK=KT.
\]

The spatial write depends on both:

\[
TX\ne XT,
\qquad
KX\ne XK.
\]

This gives a finite answer to the local ordering problem.  Order is neither a
single compulsory total order nor freely exchangeable.  The machine computes
which steps commute and which remain dependent.

The result is close to a trace-monoid discipline, but the idempotence,
involution, and additional finite transformation equations mean that the
sixteen-element monoid should not be identified with a free partially
commutative monoid.

# Part IV. Context-generated propositions

## 10. Pulling back one atom

For \(m\in M\), define

\[
P_m
=
\{s\in\widetilde S\mid x(m(s))=1\}
=m^{-1}(A_X).
\]

Although \(|M|=16\), transformations that differ in their final temporal or
constructive coordinate may induce the same spatial proposition.  Exactly
five distinct inverse images occur.

## 11. The five direct propositions

| shortest context | predicate on \((t,x,k)\) | fine support size | cost \((T,X,K)\) |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| \(\varepsilon\) | \(x\) | 8 | \((0,0,0)\) |
| \(X\) | \(t\wedge k\) | 4 | \((0,1,0)\) |
| \(KX\) | \(t\wedge\neg k\) | 4 | \((0,1,1)\) |
| \(TX\) | \(k\) | 8 | \((1,1,0)\) |
| \(TKX\) | \(\neg k\) | 8 | \((1,1,1)\) |

The table chooses one breadth-first representative.  Because \(T\) and \(K\)
commute, \(KTX\) is another length-three context for \(\neg k\).

The activation step is now part of a closed family:

\[
P_{TX}=\{k=1\}.
\]

The temporal program opens the gate, the spatial program reads the retained
construction bit, and a formerly non-spatial coordinate becomes a spatial
proposition.

## 12. Direct generation is not Boolean closure

Let

\[
\mathcal L_X^{\mathrm{ctx}}
=
\{P_m\mid m\in M\}.
\]

Then

\[
|\mathcal L_X^{\mathrm{ctx}}|=5.
\]

This family is not closed under the usual Boolean operations.  For example,

\[
\neg x\notin\mathcal L_X^{\mathrm{ctx}},
\]

\[
x\wedge k\notin\mathcal L_X^{\mathrm{ctx}},
\]

and

\[
x\vee k\notin\mathcal L_X^{\mathrm{ctx}}.
\]

Nevertheless some logical-looking operations are realized by programs.  In
particular,

\[
P_{TKX}=\neg P_{TX}.
\]

Therefore “negation is present” or “negation is absent” is too coarse.  One
must ask which predicate is negated, by which executable context, with which
ordering and resource history.

## 13. Schedule changes truth conditions

The two orders

\[
TX
\quad\text{and}\quad
XT
\]

generate different propositions:

\[
P_{TX}=\{k=1\},
\qquad
P_{XT}=\{t=1\wedge k=1\}.
\]

Likewise,

\[
P_{KX}=\{t=1\wedge k=0\},
\qquad
P_{XK}=\{t=1\wedge k=1\}.
\]

Thus exchange cannot be imported as an unconditional structural rule.  It is
valid for the pair \(T,K\) in this machine and invalid for the pairs \(T,X\)
and \(K,X\).

# Part V. Contextual quotient and finite completeness

## 14. Contextual equivalence

Define

\[
s\sim_{\mathrm{ctx}}s'
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\forall m\in M,
\;x(m(s))=x(m(s')).
\]

Equivalently, two states are related when they have the same membership
signature across the five direct propositions.

The predicates \(x\), \(k\), \(t\wedge k\), and
\(t\wedge\neg k\) jointly recover every core bit:

- \(x\) is observed directly;
- \(k\) is generated by \(TX\); and
- \(t=1\) exactly when either \(t\wedge k\) or
  \(t\wedge\neg k\) holds.

Hence all eight core states are separated.  The audit bit is never read, so
the exact partition is

\[
\widetilde S/{\sim_{\mathrm{ctx}}}
=
\bigl\{
\{(t,x,k,0),(t,x,k,1)\}
\mid(t,x,k)\in S_0
\bigr\}.
\]

## 15. Boolean envelope

Let

\[
\mathcal B_X^{\mathrm{ctx}}
=
\operatorname{Bool}(\mathcal L_X^{\mathrm{ctx}})
\]

be the smallest Boolean algebra containing the five direct propositions.

For a finite carrier, the atoms of the generated Boolean algebra are exactly
the common membership fibres of its generators.  Here those atoms are the
eight contextual classes.  Therefore

\[
\mathcal B_X^{\mathrm{ctx}}
\cong
\mathcal P(\widetilde S/{\sim_{\mathrm{ctx}}})
\cong
\mathcal P(S_0).
\]

Consequently

\[
|\mathcal B_X^{\mathrm{ctx}}|
=2^8
=256.
\]

By contrast,

\[
|\mathcal P(\widetilde S)|
=2^{16}
=65\,536.
\]

Every Boolean-envelope proposition is invariant under changing \(h\).  A
two-point core cylinder such as

\[
\{(0,0,0,0),(0,0,0,1)\}
\]

is expressible, while the fine singleton

\[
\{(0,0,0,0)\}
\]

is not.

## 16. What finite completeness means here

The Boolean envelope is extensionally complete for the finite contextual
quotient: every subset of observable equivalence classes is expressible.

It is not complete for fine identity.  Completeness is indexed by:

- the state carrier;
- the admitted program generators;
- their composition law;
- the selected atom or observer; and
- the residual channels programs may read.

If a later program gains access to \(h\), the transformation monoid changes,
the contextual partition may refine, and the proposition algebra must be
recomputed.  No absolute open-world completeness follows from the finite
calculation.

This is the precise sense in which finite construction and open interpretation
can coexist: a finite grammar may be complete for every distinction admitted
by its current interpretation while explicitly retaining an unresolved fibre.

## 17. The classical envelope forgets programs again

The 256-element Boolean algebra records only supports.  It forgets:

- which context generated a direct proposition;
- that different contexts may have the same support;
- the order of program execution;
- the shortest witness;
- the directional resource vector; and
- the checked construction history.

Thus Booleanization gives extensional completeness on the quotient by a
second act of abstraction.  It is useful, but it is not yet the proposed
proof-relevant logic on a 3-form.

# Part VI. Consequences for L/R, calibration, and logic

## 18. A local replacement for premature \(L\mid R\)

The finite monoid suggests a more disciplined local treatment of the unresolved
left/right reduction problem.

Do not first impose a total order and write every process as \(L\mid R\).
Instead:

1. represent the available steps as typed transformations;
2. compute or certify their composition relations;
3. quotient schedule words only by checked transformation equality or a
   declared observation equivalence; and
4. retain noncommuting order as semantic data.

In the fixture, \(T\) and \(K\) may exchange, while neither may freely cross
the spatial write.  The result is a dependency-sensitive order rather than a
global left/right choice.

This does not solve the general Adva \(L/R\) problem.  It identifies a finite
normal-form experiment in which order is derived from programs rather than
postulated.

## 19. Startup calibration has the same local shape

Startup calibration can be studied by the same method only after each
calibration action has a typed state transformation and a declared observer.
Then one may ask:

- which calibration steps commute;
- which orderings yield the same calibrated state;
- which distinctions are visible only after later execution; and
- which residuals remain outside the calibrated quotient.

The present equations make the analogy concrete, but do not prove that every
startup protocol reduces to this monoid or that calibration and computation
are globally identical.

## 20. Candidate semantic seeds for a logic

The fixture supplies four semantic seeds, not a natural-deduction system.

### Identity

The empty context acts as identity:

\[
P_{\varepsilon}=A_X.
\]

### Composition

Context concatenation is associative because transformation composition is
associative.  This is a semantic candidate for sequential cut, not yet a proof
term or cut-elimination theorem.

### Ordered structure

Exchange is justified exactly where a checked commutation equation exists.
The machine supplies \(TK=KT\), but rejects unconditional exchange.

### Observer-indexed propositions

Propositions arise by program pullback from a declared atom.  The direct
language is generated by what the machine can make observable, not selected
arbitrarily from the fine powerset.

## 21. A tempting transporter question and its boundary

Support inclusion gives a finite semantic preorder:

\[
A\subseteq B.
\]

One might ask:

> When \(A\subseteq B\), is there an admitted program \(F\) that uniformly
> transports every realization of \(A\) into a realization of \(B\)?

If \(F\) is merely another state transformation, however, this condition is
not stricter.  The identity witnesses every inclusion:

\[
\operatorname{id}(A)=A\subseteq B.
\]

Moreover, a state-changing program may transport non-included predicates,
such as \(k\) into \(\neg k\).  The exact no-go computation is recorded in
[`0052-finite-state-transport-proof-no-go.md`](0052-finite-state-transport-proof-no-go.md).

The genuinely proof-relevant next question requires proposition-indexed
evidence carriers and an admitted grammar of transformations between them.
That evidence layer must be introduced before implication syntax or a
natural-deduction claim.

# Part VII. Verification and limits

## 22. What the fixture establishes

Within the declared finite machine, the fixture establishes:

1. Rust-checked scalar realizations of the three Boolean coordinate updates;
2. an exact sixteen-element transformation monoid, closed under composition;
3. shortest representatives of length at most four for every transformation;
4. the equations \(T^2=T\), \(X^2=X\), \(K^2=1\), and \(TK=KT\);
5. the noncommutations \(TX\ne XT\) and \(KX\ne XK\);
6. exact identity and concatenation/composition laws for context words;
7. exactly five direct spatial propositions generated from one atom;
8. a shortest context and directional step-count vector for every direct
   proposition;
9. explicit failure of complement, intersection, and union closure for the
   direct family;
10. exactly eight contextual equivalence classes, each retaining two audit
    histories;
11. a 256-element Boolean envelope equal to the powerset of the contextual
    quotient; and
12. strict separation of that envelope from the 65,536-element fine
    powerset.

## 23. What it does not establish

The fixture does not establish:

- a stable Adva context or transformation-monoid API;
- that \(T,X,K\) are canonical three-computer instructions;
- a universal interpreter or U0 uniform programmability;
- a complete presentation of the sixteen-element monoid by generators and
  relations;
- completeness for an infinite or open world;
- activation of actual Adva history by an Adva program;
- a natural-deduction calculus;
- implication, discharge, binding, or cut elimination;
- proof-relevant entailment;
- a solution to the general \(L/R\) problem;
- equivalence of startup calibration and ordinary execution;
- an irreducible triadic cocycle; or
- mathematical novelty of the finite automata and Boolean-algebra facts.

Finite transformation monoids, automata minimization, inverse-image predicate
transformers, and finite Boolean algebras are established mathematics.  The
research value here is their disciplined placement between the triadic
program, observer, residual, and logic questions.

## 24. Conservative conclusion

The local finite result is:

> Three ordered program generators close to a finite transformation monoid.
> Their pullbacks of one spatial atom generate a small direct proposition
> family.  Booleanization is complete exactly on the quotient separated by
> all admitted contexts, while unreadable audit residuals remain outside that
> completeness.

This clarifies the finite-representation problem without pretending to finish
it.  A finite observer does not obtain an absolute description of an open
world.  It obtains a complete language for a declared contextual quotient,
plus an explicit residual recording what that language still cannot say.
