# Frontier transport and the first interchange law

Status: bounded research calibration
Date: 2026-08-29
Depends on: `0005-causal-cut-alexandrov-topology.md`
Executable witness: `tests/python/test_shared_expression_causal_topology.py`

## Question

The causal-open topology assigns a source-decorated frontier

\[
  U\longmapsto\partial_D U
\]

to every completed past \(U\).  A static assignment is not yet geometry.  The
next question is how the frontier changes along an inclusion \(U\subseteq V\),
and whether two causal paths between the same opens agree, differ coherently,
or leave a residual.

This calibration constructs the smallest executable transport law directly
from checked wires and exhausts every schedule of the five-operation shared
fork--recombine program.

## Elementary frontier transport

Let \(U\) be a completed past and let \(e\notin U\) be enabled, meaning every
immediate predecessor of \(e\) is already in \(U\).  Then

\[
  U'=U\cup\{e\}
\]

is again a completed past.  The cover \(U\subset U'\) carries an elementary
frontier step:

1. checked frontier wires consumed by \(e\) disappear;
2. checked outputs of \(e\) crossing into the remaining future appear;
3. every unrelated frontier wire is retained;
4. source identifiers are transported by the Rust operation lineage rule.

Thus an enabled event is read simultaneously as a temporal computation and a
spatial replacement of boundary ports.  The step is derived from one checked
diagram; it is not a Python-defined program operation.

A causal schedule

\[
  U=U_0\subset U_1\subset\cdots\subset U_k=V
\]

composes these elementary replacements into a `FrontierTransport`.  The
schedule remains part of the transport trace even when its endpoints agree
with another schedule.

## Independent events form a square

Suppose two distinct events \(e\) and \(f\) are both enabled at \(U\), and
their elementary steps consume disjoint frontier wires.  Linear use in the
checked diagram then makes the two events independent at this cut.  Both
orders are legal:

\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
 & U & \\
 e\swarrow & & \searrow f \\
 U\cup\{e\} & & U\cup\{f\} \\
 f\searrow & & \swarrow e \\
 & U\cup\{e,f\}. &
\end{array}
\]

The executable `InterchangeWitness` checks four facts:

1. both events are enabled at the same starting cut;
2. their consumed frontier wires are disjoint;
3. both two-step schedules are accepted;
4. their final completed past and full source-decorated frontier are equal.

This is the first bounded interchange law.  It states that independent
boundary replacements form a filled square.  It does not state that the
arithmetic operations commute, nor does it create a stable semantic
`CoherenceCell`.

## Exhausting the branch schedules

The witness has the causal shape

\[
  \text{root copy}
  \longrightarrow
  \begin{cases}
    \text{left negation},\\
    \text{right copy}\longrightarrow\text{right multiply},
  \end{cases}
  \longrightarrow
  \text{recombining add}.
\]

The root copy is forced first and the final add is forced last.  The left
negation can occur before, between, or after the dependent right-copy/multiply
pair.  Hence the checked event order has exactly three linear extensions.

The experiment enumerates all three schedules.  Their operation histories are
distinct, but their initial and terminal frontiers agree.  The graph whose
edges exchange adjacent independent events is connected, and every edge is
validated by an `InterchangeWitness`.  In this finite witness, all schedule
differences are therefore generated by the local interchange square.

Each elementary step also preserves source support.  Copy changes one
occurrence into two; multiplication and addition can combine several
occurrences into one wire; nevertheless the set of checked sources crossing
the step is unchanged.

## Bounded coherence and its no-go

The positive result is

\[
  \boxed{
  \text{independent causal schedules}
  \xRightarrow{\text{interchange squares}}
  \text{one extensional frontier transport}.
  }
\]

The accompanying no-go is equally important:

> Equal initial and terminal frontiers do not imply equal schedule history.

The paths remain distinct checked traces.  They become equivalent only after
an explicit interchange witness accounts for their difference.  Endpoint
equality alone still cannot manufacture a program equation or erase history.

## Why ordinary concurrency is still flat

The branch introduced genuine incomparable events, but no holonomy residual
appears.  Every alternative schedule is connected to every other by swaps of
independent steps, and those swaps close on the same decorated frontier.
Ordinary scheduling freedom inside one deterministic acyclic program is
therefore flat in this bounded sense.

This rules out a tempting but premature conclusion: branching alone does not
produce curvature.  A nontrivial residual must involve more than reordering
disjoint work.  Plausible next sources are:

- competing rewrites that overlap on the same program region;
- objectification or forgetting that identifies endpoints while retaining
  different internal transports;
- chart transitions with nontrivial boundary re-expression;
- guarded feedback or recursion, once it has a checked semantics.

## Stable checkpoint

The three calibrations now supply a coherent finite picture:

1. a checked program has temporal evaluation and inverse-neighborhood
   readings;
2. its event order generates a non-Boolean Alexandrov topology of completed
   pasts;
3. every completed past has a typed source-decorated frontier;
4. enabled events transport those frontiers;
5. independent transports obey a checked local interchange law;
6. schedule history remains visible until an explicit witness relates it.

This is a stable research boundary.  The implementation remains test-local,
and no general topology, transport, or coherence API is promoted.  The next
phase should introduce one carefully bounded overlapping rewrite rather than
adding more deterministic DAG examples.
