## Exact interface and elementary argument

The source action is generated by a and b and their inverses A and B on
P1(F_p), p = 5 or 7. The original generators are

    a = [[1,1],[0,1]], b = [[1,0],[1,1]].

The primary coordinate changes are

    H = [[1,2],[1,1]], K = [[1,1],[0,1]].

Composition is left-after-right, so the composite is K H, not H K.
The first leg transports A to B; the second leg transports B to C. These
capital scope names are local labels, distinct from the generator letters.

The intermediate frame contains the prime field, the named ordered generator
alphabet, its two source generator matrices, the local scope label, ordered
probes, and the predicate subset. The four inverses are determined by the two
invertible generators. Every leg separately checks all four actions on the
whole projective carrier and complete observation-row coverage.

The matrices in an interface are compared projectively: divide by the first
nonzero entry in the finite field. This is allowed scalar equivalence, not
arbitrary rewriting. Probe order is significant; predicate subset order is
not. The parser and per-leg checker are exactly the parent's version zero.

Given exact matches at B,

    rho_B(g) H = H rho_A(g),
    rho_C(g) K = K rho_B(g)

imply

    rho_C(g) K H = K H rho_A(g).

The same chaining applies to probes and to predicates transported by inverse
image. Checks on the four generator letters compose to all finite words by
the elementary induction in the parent note. We neither enumerate all words
nor claim anything about arbitrary interpreters.

Both H and K must be invertible. Their determinants are checked before any
field inverse; projective inverses use adjugates. Matrix order is never
replaced by commutative multiplication. No zero additive residual is used to
justify division by zero. Equality of the final point action is not an
identification of source occurrences, records or paths.

## Callable receiving profile

`compose(receipts, expected, route_ids, fuel=None)` in `compose.py` takes two
parent-profile receipts, two independently selected expected contexts, and two
distinct bounded research-local route labels. These labels retain provenance;
they are not authenticated history identifiers or native OccurrenceIds.

1. Validate both legs with the unchanged parent checker.
2. Derive the first target frame and second source frame from validated data.
3. Compare every declared frame field.
4. If they match, construct and check the direct receipt with K H.
5. Compare final frames and retain direct/staged pointwise squares plus both
   route labels, all three scopes and both ordered coordinate changes.

The expected contexts are supplied by the receiver, not taken on trust from
the incoming receipts. Nevertheless, choosing suitable contexts still needs
an interpretation of the actual task. This mechanism cannot certify that a
human meant that interpretation.

| Outcome | Meaning |
|---|---|
| AcceptedComposition | both complete legs and the declared intermediate interface match |
| InvalidIntermediateInterface | both legs pass, but explicit middle fields disagree |
| BlockedLeg | a leg is false, malformed, uncovered or resource-limited; its reason remains attached |
| InvalidRouteLabels | route labels fail this finite profile, including a duplicate label |
| UnknownResource | the shared work/time budget is exhausted before admission |

All outputs explicitly deny native admission. The callable function is a
bounded research prototype, not a hardened public JSON service. The parent
JSON constraints apply to the two legs and expected contexts; no separate
general-purpose network protocol is introduced.

## Results: successful calculations on incompatible interfaces

Each field has twelve declared cases, **24 total**:

- Matched composition passes.
- Four independently accepted second legs change exactly one middle field:
  scope, ordered probes, predicate, or generator assignment. All four are
  rejected at the seam, while the composed coordinate matrix K H is unchanged.
- Multiplying both middle generator representatives by 2 and reversing the
  predicate list passes. This preserves projective actions and the predicate
  set, so a raw JSON byte comparison would be unnecessarily restrictive.
- Removing second-leg infinity observations blocks composition with retained
  `UnknownCoverage`.
- A forged observed value blocks composition with `InvalidEvidence`.
- Duplicate route labels are rejected.
- Zero fuel returns `UnknownResource` without a per-leg budget reset.
- K = H^-1 gives identity coordinate action while preserving two steps and
  the three distinct A/B/C scopes.
- Wrong-order K H versus H K is separated by exact projective points.

For example, in F_5 the first leg's ordered target probes are `[2,4,1]`.
A second leg starting from `[1,4,2]` is internally consistent and passes its
own receiver, yet changes the meaning of the observation coordinates. The
composition checker reports `ordered_probes`, not a false arithmetic claim.

The correct normalized product in F_5 is `[1,4,3,3]`; the wrong order gives
`[1,3,1,2]`, and all six projective points distinguish them. In F_7 the
corresponding products are `[1,5,4,4]` and `[1,3,1,2]`; six of eight points
distinguish them. Thus neither commutativity nor uniform separation was
assumed.

Six accepted compositions across the fields retain **168 direct/staged point
squares**. F_7 is the fresh-domain reuse of the same receiver and frame rules,
not just a repeat of F_5's numeric values. The primary and a fresh child-process
replay have identical non-timing evidence. Reversing the stored ordered route
history in one comparison control is detected.

No old 4774-word suite, 31824-pair group table or Zot search was rerun. We
exercise the per-leg validator as a dependency, not its old exhaustive suite.
