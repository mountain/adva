# Contextual Beta-Ledger Transport and Linear Strong Normalization

Status: research-local principal-implication-detour transformation,
replayable heterogeneous ledger transport, contextual closure, and strong
normalization theorem following
[0084](0084-threaded-natural-deduction-entailment-cell.md) and
[0085](0085-ordered-substitution-cut-beta-ledger-boundary.md).

No stable Adva beta event, proof position, proof-node identity, ledger
transport, reduction relation, normal form, logical symbol, or Rust API is
introduced here. The executable companion remains a self-contained Python
research fixture over the three rules of \(\mathrm{TND}_0\).

The word **beta** in this note names contraction of a principal implication
detour in a natural-deduction proof tree:

\[
\operatorname E
\bigl(\operatorname I_x^b(\pi),\sigma\bigr).
\]

It is beta-like in the proof-theoretic and Curry--Howard sense. The fixture
has no term language or evaluator, so this note does not claim a runtime
lambda-calculus reduction theorem.

## 0. Executive result

Let

\[
\pi:\Gamma\circ x:A\vdash_K B
\blacktriangleright\Lambda_\pi,
\qquad
\sigma:\Delta\vdash_K A
\blacktriangleright\Lambda_\sigma
\]

be the body and argument of a checked principal implication detour

\[
R=\operatorname E
\bigl(\operatorname I_x^b(\pi),\sigma\bigr).
\]

Checkedness of \(R\), rather than an additional assumption, implies the
strict V0 freshness interface required by ordered substitution. Therefore
note 0085 constructs the checked contractum

\[
C=\operatorname{Sub}_x(\pi,\sigma).
\]

If the unique target position in the body ledger gives

\[
\Lambda_\pi
=L\circ[x^{\mathrm{open}}]\circ Q,
\]

then every record in \(Q\) is discharged and the two complete ledgers are

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_R
=L\circ[x^{\mathrm{discharged},b}]
\circ Q\circ\Lambda_\sigma
\quad\leadsto_\beta\quad
\Lambda_C
=L\circ\Lambda_\sigma\circ Q.
}
\tag{B0}
\]

The transformation is not ledger equality. It retires exactly the bound
target record and moves the argument block across a discharged audit suffix.
A replayable `RootBetaLedgerTransport` records a span of all surviving
resource identities between the two ordered boundaries.

The root transformation lifts through every proof context generated by
right implication introduction and either premise of right implication
elimination. For a checked derivation \(D\) and a premise-index path \(p\)
selecting a principal detour, the contextual operation computes

\[
\boxed{
D:\Gamma\vdash_K A\blacktriangleright\Lambda
\quad\Longrightarrow_{\beta,p}^{\tau}\quad
D':\Gamma\vdash_K A\blacktriangleright\Lambda',
}
\tag{SR}
\]

where \(\tau\) is a checked heterogeneous ledger transport. Conclusion and
ordered open context are equal; complete ledgers are related by \(\tau\), not
identified.

Every such step satisfies

\[
|\Lambda'|=|\Lambda|-1,
\qquad
\operatorname{dchg}(\Lambda')
=\operatorname{dchg}(\Lambda)-1,
\tag{M1}
\]

and, for the current proof-tree representation,

\[
\operatorname{nodes}(D')
=\operatorname{nodes}(D)-3.
\tag{M2}
\]

Consequently the beta-like reduction relation of finite \(\mathrm{TND}_0\)
proof trees is strongly normalizing. A reduction sequence has length at most
the initial number of discharged ledger records.

This note alone does not establish confluence, eta or permutative conversions,
runtime subject reduction, or normalization for a future calculus with
recursion, contraction, source transfer, quantifiers, or explicit cuts. Note
[0087](0087-beta-history-local-confluence-audit-2-cells.md) subsequently proves
local and global confluence for this beta-only proof-tree relation and a unique
beta normal form for each fixed finite starting derivation. It does not make
distinct audit histories equal or install a proof-coherence quotient.

## 1. Position in the proof-theoretic tradition

In Prawitz-style natural deduction, an introduction immediately consumed by
the corresponding elimination is a maximal-formula detour. Contracting it is
the natural-deduction counterpart of reducing a principal cut in a Gentzen
presentation. Under Curry--Howard, the same shape motivates the name beta.

The three formulations are related but are not definitionally the same
proof object:

\[
\begin{array}{c}
\text{principal natural-deduction detour}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{cut-reduction analogue}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{term-level beta analogue}.
\end{array}
\]

Only the first line is represented in the current fixture. Note 0085 first
proved ordered one-hole substitution and conditional fresh-cut
admissibility. The present note now uses that theorem to define reduction at
the root, closes it under proof contexts, and obtains a decreasing measure.

This order is important. A proposed rewrite is not a proof-theoretic
reduction until its output is again a checked derivation and its resource
boundary has a declared transformation.

## 2. Freshness reflects through discharge

Write \(D_{x,b}(\Lambda)\) for the operation that replaces the unique open
use of \(x\) by its discharged use carrying binder \(b\).

### Lemma 2.1: discharge-freshness reflection

Suppose

\[
R=\operatorname E
\bigl(\operatorname I_x^b(\pi),\sigma\bigr)
\]

is checked. Then

\[
\operatorname{Fresh}_{V0}(\pi,\sigma,x).
\tag{FR}
\]

### Proof

Checked implication introduction establishes all of the following:

1. the body is checked;
2. \(x\) is the unique right-boundary open occurrence;
3. its formula is the premise formula of the introduced implication;
4. \(b\) is absent from the body ledger; and
5. the abstraction ledger is exactly \(D_{x,b}(\Lambda_\pi)\).

Recursive ledger validation also makes the target's open `ResourceUse`
unique in the complete body ledger, not merely rightmost in the open
projection.

Checked elimination establishes exact formula agreement with the conclusion
of \(\sigma\) and successful merging of

\[
D_{x,b}(\Lambda_\pi)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\Lambda_\sigma.
\]

Discharge changes neither scoped occurrence identities nor source
identities. It only changes the target status and adds the already fresh
binder \(b\). Hence successful merging after discharge implies disjoint
occurrences and sources before discharge. The binder set of
\(\Lambda_\pi\) is a subset of that of
\(D_{x,b}(\Lambda_\pi)\), so binder disjointness also reflects. These are
exactly the strict V0 interface conditions of note 0085. \(\square\)

Thus a checked principal detour cannot legitimately produce a pre-graft
aperture. If the executable contraction finds an aperture, formula mismatch,
or freshness rejection after recognizing a checked redex, it reports an
internal substitution-invariant failure. The aperture result of note 0085
remains meaningful for an attempted graft that was not already the argument
of a checked elimination.

## 3. Exact root transport

Let \(i\) be the unique position of \(x^{\mathrm{open}}\) in
\(\Lambda_\pi\), and write

\[
L=\Lambda_\pi[:i],
\qquad
Q=\Lambda_\pi[i+1:].
\]

Because the open projection of \(\Lambda_\pi\) is
\(\Gamma\circ x:A\) and \(x\) is its right boundary, every record in
\(Q\) is discharged. The implication introduction and elimination rules
therefore produce

\[
\Lambda_R
=L\circ[x^b]\circ Q\circ S,
\qquad
S:=\Lambda_\sigma,
\tag{R}
\]

while ordered substitution produces

\[
\Lambda_C=L\circ S\circ Q.
\tag{C}
\]

Here \([x^b]\) abbreviates the single discharged target record with binder
\(b\). No other record is deleted, duplicated, relabelled, or alpha-renamed.

### 3.1 The survivor span

Let \(\kappa(u)\) be the scoped occurrence key of a resource use. Checked
ledgers make these keys unique. Define

\[
\mathsf{Surv}
=\operatorname{Keys}(\Lambda_R)\setminus\{\kappa(x)\}.
\]

Then

\[
\mathsf{Surv}=\operatorname{Keys}(\Lambda_C),
\]

and the transport is represented by the span

\[
\Lambda_R
\xleftarrow{\ e_-\ }
\mathsf{Surv}
\xrightarrow{\ e_+\ }
\Lambda_C.
\tag{T}
\]

The maps send each surviving identity to its position on the corresponding
ledger boundary. The map \(e_-\) is injective with complement exactly the
retired target position; \(e_+\) is bijective. At the two selected positions
the complete `ResourceUse` values agree.

This is a survivor partial bijection, not an order isomorphism of the full
ledgers. Let

\[
\ell=|L|,
\qquad q=|Q|,
\qquad s=|S|.
\]

The induced input-to-output position map is

\[
\phi(j)=
\begin{cases}
j,&j<\ell,\\
\text{undefined},&j=\ell,\\
j+s-1,&\ell<j\le\ell+q,\\
j-q-1,&j\ge\ell+q+1.
\end{cases}
\tag{P}
\]

It preserves the internal order of each of the \(L\), \(Q\), and \(S\)
blocks. Every cross-pair consisting of one \(Q\) record and one \(S\)
record reverses order. Empty blocks are permitted.

### 3.2 Open order is nevertheless preserved

The block \(Q\) contains only discharged records, and \(x^b\) is also
discharged. Moving \(S\) across \(Q\) therefore does not exchange two open
hypotheses. The restriction of the survivor span to open records is an order
isomorphism, and in fact

\[
\operatorname{OpenContext}(R)
=\Gamma\circ\Delta
=\operatorname{OpenContext}(C).
\]

Thus beta transport does not install an exchange rule in \(\mathrm{TND}_0\).
If the crossing later receives geometric semantics, only the surviving
\(Q/S\) part may be described as a braid-like crossing. The entire event is
not a braid: it also retires \(x^b\).

### 3.3 Authority retirement

Strict freshness and complete-ledger uniqueness imply more than absence of
the target occurrence. In the contractum ledger there is no remaining record
with

- the target scoped occurrence identity;
- the target linear source identity; or
- binder identity \(b\).

This is authority retirement in the current proof, not deletion from the
transformation history.

## 4. Current proof state and audit history remain separate

The result of contraction is a new current proof tree. Its ledger describes
the resource uses still occurring in that tree. The target assumption leaf,
the introducing binder node, and the eliminating detour node no longer occur,
so the retired target record must not remain in the current ledger.

The beta certificate separately retains:

- the proof before contraction;
- the selected detour and proof position;
- the target occurrence and binder;
- the argument and graft site;
- the proof after contraction; and
- all survivor transports.

This is the same controlled-forgetting discipline identified by note 0085:

\[
\text{current dependency state}
\ne
\text{cross-state transformation history}.
\]

Making the current ledger append-only by retaining \(x^b\) would no longer
describe the contractum proof. Conversely, discarding the beta certificate
would make the history unreplayable. A reduction history is therefore a
sequence of external transport cells whose endpoints are current proofs.

This separation is also essential to the normalization measure below. If
retired records were copied into each new current ledger as historical
events, ledger length would cease to measure proof reduction.

## 5. Proof positions and the root certificate

The current `NDProof` fixture has recursive tree structure but no nominal
proof-node identities. A proof occurrence is therefore addressed by a root
proof together with a premise-index path

\[
p\in\{0,1\}^{<\omega}.
\]

The valid path grammar is more restrictive than an arbitrary bit string:

- an implication-introduction node admits only premise index \(0\);
- an implication-elimination node admits indices \(0\) and \(1\); and
- an assumption node admits no further index.

A path is relative to the exact input root. It is not a stable name that may
be reused after the proof changes. The executable checker also rejects
negative indices, non-integers, and Boolean values masquerading as integers.

At a selected root the proof must have the exact shape

\[
\operatorname E
\left(
  \operatorname I_x^b(\pi),
  \sigma
\right).
\]

The substitution trace determines a unique relative path \(r\) from
\(\pi\) to the target assumption leaf. If the beta redex occurs at global
path \(p\), then the relevant proof occurrences are

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{abstraction before:}&p\circ(0),\\
\text{body before:}&p\circ(0,0),\\
\text{target leaf before:}&p\circ(0,0)\circ r,\\
\text{argument before:}&p\circ(1),\\
\text{contractum after:}&p,\\
\text{argument graft after:}&p\circ r.
\end{array}
\tag{Paths}
\]

The elimination node, introduction node, and target assumption leaf have no
surviving proof-node occurrence. The argument is grafted exactly once.
Off-spine body siblings are reused structurally; nodes on the substitution
spine and all ancestors of the selected redex are rebuilt. Without nominal
`ProofNodeId` values these statements are path correspondences, not claims of
node identity.

### 5.1 Replayable root data

A research-local root certificate retains or deterministically derives:

- a typed beta event identifier;
- redex, abstraction, body, argument, and contractum proofs;
- the open and retired target uses, with target occurrence and binder derived
  from the abstraction and retired use;
- the replayable ordered-substitution certificate;
- the target leaf path;
- exact \(L\), \(Q\), and \(S\) block boundaries;
- local survivor embeddings on both ledgers;
- the boundary context and conclusion.

Replay recomputes the contractum from the redex. It does not accept an
asserted output, block permutation, target path, or survivor map. A typed
event wrapper prevents empty display identities, but globally allocated
event identity remains deferred. Replay also verifies separately that the
retired occurrence, source, and binder are absent from the result ledger.

## 6. Contextual closure

Proof contexts with one distinguished proof hole are generated by

\[
\mathcal C[-]::=
[-]
\mid
\operatorname I_y^c(\mathcal C[-])
\mid
\operatorname E(\mathcal C[-],\rho)
\mid
\operatorname E(\rho,\mathcal C[-]),
\tag{PC}
\]

provided the source filling is checked. A premise-index path is the
executable address of such a context.

Suppose a local transport relates

\[
D:\Gamma\vdash A\blacktriangleright\Lambda
\quad\text{and}\quad
D':\Gamma\vdash A\blacktriangleright\Lambda'.
\]

It lifts through the three possible ancestor frames.

### 6.1 Introduction frame

If the source ancestor is

\[
\operatorname I_y^c(D),
\]

then \(y\) is the right-boundary open occurrence of \(D\). Boundary
preservation makes it the same right-boundary occurrence of \(D'\). It cannot
be the retired target, because the former is open at this frame and the
latter is already discharged inside the selected redex. Rebuild

\[
\operatorname I_y^c(D').
\]

The ledger-position map lifts without changing indices, but payloads must be
recomputed after discharge. In particular, \(y\) may belong to an argument
block that moved across a discharged suffix. Both endpoints must contain the
same newly discharged \(y^c\) use at their respective survivor positions.

### 6.2 Elimination function frame

If the source ancestor is

\[
\operatorname E(D,\rho),
\]

replace only its function premise and rebuild

\[
\operatorname E(D',\rho).
\]

The sibling ledger is appended unchanged. If the transported child has
source length \(n\), result length \(n-1\), and the sibling index is \(k\),
then its global position changes from \(n+k\) to \(n-1+k\).

### 6.3 Elimination argument frame

If the source ancestor is

\[
\operatorname E(\rho,D),
\]

replace only its argument premise and rebuild

\[
\operatorname E(\rho,D').
\]

The function ledger is an unchanged prefix. If its length is \(m\), the
child survivor map and retired index are shifted by \(m\).

### 6.4 Why rebuilding remains admissible

The local contractum has the same conclusion and open context as the redex.
It contains a permutation and proper subset of the redex resource identities.
Consequently:

- an introduction sees the same right-boundary principal and no new binder;
- an elimination still sees the exact premise formula; and
- a formerly disjoint sibling cannot acquire a new occurrence, source, or
  binder collision.

Thus every ancestor constructor that checked before contraction also checks
after contraction.

## 7. Heterogeneous ledger-indexed subject reduction

Define `RedexAt(D,p)` to mean that the valid input-relative path \(p\)
selects a principal implication detour in the checked proof \(D\).

### Theorem 7.1: contextual beta-transport

If

\[
D:\Gamma\vdash_K A\blacktriangleright\Lambda
\]

is checked and `RedexAt(D,p)`, then deterministic contraction computes a
checked proof and a replayable certificate

\[
D':\Gamma\vdash_K A\blacktriangleright\Lambda',
\qquad
\tau:\Lambda\leadsto_{\beta,p}\Lambda'.
\]

The certificate establishes all of the following:

1. the result conclusion is exactly \(A\);
2. the result open context is exactly \(\Gamma\), with the same order;
3. exactly one discharged target record is retired;
4. the retired occurrence, source, and binder authority are absent from
   \(\Lambda'\);
5. every other scoped occurrence has exactly one source and result position;
6. corresponding surviving `ResourceUse` values at the outer boundary are
   equal;
7. the global survivor map is uniquely determined by contextual rebuilding
   and replay, rather than an asserted arbitrary permutation; and
8. replay from \((D,p)\) reconstructs exactly \(D'\), every ancestor frame,
   and the full certificate.

### Proof

The root case is Lemma 2.1 followed by the ordered-substitution theorem of
note 0085 and the exact ledger calculation of Section 3. Induct on the length
of \(p\), equivalently on the surrounding proof context. The three inductive
cases are the introduction, elimination-function, and elimination-argument
frames of Section 6. Each constructor preserves checkedness, conclusion, and
open context, and each has a deterministic lift of the survivor positions.
No other rules exist in \(\mathrm{TND}_0\). \(\square\)

The theorem is deliberately heterogeneous. It does not claim

\[
\Gamma\vdash A\blacktriangleright\Lambda
=
\Gamma\vdash A\blacktriangleright\Lambda'.
\]

It supplies a directed transformation between two differently indexed
checked judgments. Erasing \(\tau\) retains ordinary boundary-sequent
preservation but loses the proof-relevant thread movement.

## 8. Linear strong normalization

Define the primary reduction measure

\[
\mu(D)
=
\operatorname{dchg}(\Lambda_D)
=
\bigl|
\{u\in\Lambda_D:u.\operatorname{status}=\mathrm{discharged}\}
\bigr|.
\]

### Lemma 8.1: exact ledger descent

For every certified contextual beta step

\[
D\Longrightarrow_{\beta,p}D',
\]

one has

\[
|\Lambda_{D'}|=|\Lambda_D|-1,
\qquad
\mu(D')=\mu(D)-1.
\]

### Proof

At the root, the transport removes exactly \(x^b\) and preserves every other
record and status. An introduction ancestor discharges the same one surviving
open record on both sides, so it increases both measures equally. Either
elimination ancestor concatenates the same sibling ledger on both sides.
Induction over the proof-position path proves the equations at the outer
root. \(\square\)

There is also an independent proof-tree measure. Let

\[
\operatorname{nodes}(D)
=1+\sum_{P\in\operatorname{premises}(D)}\operatorname{nodes}(P).
\]

Linearity makes substitution graft \(\sigma\) exactly once. If \(r\) is the
unique target assumption leaf, then

\[
\operatorname{nodes}(C)
=\operatorname{nodes}(\pi)-1
+\operatorname{nodes}(\sigma),
\]

whereas

\[
\operatorname{nodes}(R)
=\operatorname{nodes}(\pi)
+\operatorname{nodes}(\sigma)+2.
\]

Hence each contextual step removes exactly the elimination node,
introduction node, and target assumption leaf:

\[
\operatorname{nodes}(D')=\operatorname{nodes}(D)-3.
\]

This secondary measure is specific to the present unfolded proof-tree
representation. The complete current ledger is the more intrinsic V0
measure and remains preferable if proof storage later uses sharing.

### Theorem 8.2: strong normalization of beta-like \(\mathrm{TND}_0\)

There is no infinite sequence

\[
D_0\Longrightarrow_\beta D_1
\Longrightarrow_\beta D_2
\Longrightarrow_\beta\cdots
\]

of certified principal-implication-detour contractions between finite checked
\(\mathrm{TND}_0\) proof trees.

### Proof

Lemma 8.1 would turn such a sequence into an infinite strictly descending
sequence

\[
\mu(D_0)>\mu(D_1)>\mu(D_2)>\cdots
\]

of natural numbers, which is impossible. \(\square\)

Every reduction sequence therefore has length at most \(\mu(D_0)\). The
independent proof-node measure also gives the bound

\[
\left\lfloor
\frac{\operatorname{nodes}(D_0)-1}{3}
\right\rfloor.
\]

Since a finite proof tree has finitely many proof positions, repeatedly
selecting any available principal detour reaches a beta normal form. This
establishes both strong and weak normalization for the declared beta-only
relation.

This theorem alone does not establish that two different reduction choices
reach the same normal form. Note
[0087](0087-beta-history-local-confluence-audit-2-cells.md) subsequently proves
that fact for reductions from one fixed starting proof; it does not identify
arbitrary proofs of the same sequent.

## 9. What normalization does and does not halt

Within the current fragment, a single reduction step is a finite checked
operation, redex enumeration is a finite tree scan, and every sequence of
such steps terminates. Therefore none of the following is an \(\Omega\)
boundary:

- absence of a redex at one requested path;
- rejection of an invalid proof position;
- completion of a beta-normal-form scan; or
- the finite history of beta transports.

This is an important constraint on the earlier language of exploration. A
finite vocabulary alone never guaranteed termination, but strict linearity
has now supplied a genuine decreasing invariant for this particular process.

Nor does the family of reductions from one finite proof require a
compactification boundary. Its height is bounded by \(\mu(D_0)\), so it has
no infinite ray. Compactification becomes relevant only after introducing at
least one genuinely unbounded source, for example:

1. recursive proof or program constructors;
2. open proof search whose task family is not finitely exhausted; or
3. an unbounded compatible family of finite starting proofs or reduction
   histories.

Any future compactification must respect the grammar already exposed here:
typed proof positions, open ports, surviving thread identities, authority
retirement, and explicit transport cells must remain visible until a declared
observer quotient specifies which history may be forgotten.

## 11. Proven, calibrated, and deferred

### Proven in the mathematical fragment

- freshness reflection from a checked principal detour;
- exact root transport
  \(L[x^b]QS\leadsto LSQ\);
- the survivor span with one retired target authority;
- preservation of each survivor and of ordered open context;
- contextual closure through all three possible ancestor frames;
- heterogeneous ledger-indexed subject reduction;
- exact ledger and proof-tree descent; and
- strong normalization of finite beta-like \(\mathrm{TND}_0\).

### Executably calibrated

- deterministic root and arbitrary-position contraction;
- exact block decompositions and survivor positions;
- a target-leaf path derived from substitution replay;
- ancestor-frame reconstruction;
- authority-retirement and boundary checks;
- full certificate replay and forgery rejection; and
- the two independent decreasing measures.

### Deferred

- stable nominal `ProofNodeId`, `BetaEventId`, and proof-version identity;
- a stable promoted composition API for successive transport certificates;
- equality or a quotient of distinct audit histories;
- uniqueness of history 2-cells and higher coherence laws;
- eta and commuting or permutative conversions;
- a term AST, capture-avoiding term substitution, and runtime beta semantics;
- a sequent calculus with explicit cuts and a cut-elimination theorem;
- alpha-rebasing, source transfer, and aperture-filling transformations;
- proof-relevant quantifiers and eigenvariable transport;
- normalization for recursion, nonlinear structural rules, or a larger
  connective language;
- a justified quotient identifying proofs connected by transports; and
- compactification or \(\Omega\) claims for genuinely unbounded histories.

## 12. Subsequent proof-theoretic discharge

Strong normalization answers whether every beta-like exploration stops. Note
[0087](0087-beta-history-local-confluence-audit-2-cells.md) subsequently
answers the proof-tree confluence question. It separates strict endpoint-tree
equality, extensional transport composition, literal history equality, and
explicit history 2-cells; treats independent and nested local peaks; proves
local confluence; and combines it with this note's strong normalization to
obtain global confluence.

Consequently every fixed finite starting `TND0` derivation has one beta normal
form under strict proof-tree equality. This is not a claim that arbitrary
proofs of the same sequent have the same normal form. Nor does confluence make
different contraction histories literally equal. A global history equality,
an observer-relative proof-coherence quotient, and uniqueness of higher cells
remain separate obligations.

Recursion, open exploration, and compactification likewise remain separate
extensions rather than consequences of the terminating, confluent linear
beta core.
